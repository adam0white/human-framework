# The camp: a continuing story

The new `/camp/` page selects camp host 0.2.0 and story host 0.1.0 around frozen Human/runtime 0.1.1. It is one actual camp followed by a supply window and continued camp life. Original Common Ground, Before the rain and the other experimental games keep their existing routes, rules and saves.

## Ordinary play

Begin with four visible work choices and a recommended woodshed stage. Every project remains selectable; the introduction does not lock valid alternatives. A completed project changes the next recommendation. The same HUD, five keyboard-accessible panels and work/time controls persist throughout the story.

Available people recover without a finite rest assignment. Next Event stops at an actual work/recovery/readiness boundary; a short review interval is the fallback when nobody is assigned. A player who becomes able to work at minute 77 is not silently advanced to Meryem's minute-96 completion. Play uses paid whole-minute steps, pauses for new work/readiness or chapter decisions, and pauses when reading panels/disclosures or leaving the visible page. Browser Pause preserves work; Stop changes the assignment.

Construction retains its physical fraction and material reservation when stopped. Its workbench benefit applies to the remaining work; same-worker stop/resume does not refresh the skill/tool basis. A willing, capable replacement pays only their future work. The People panel offers a project request, an owned eight-minute meal and explicit handover requests. Meryem chooses how to pursue accepted work, including food and recovery. Fixed gathering still yields only when completed; released project gathering can therefore lose its unfinished output. This is an explicit retained host abstraction, not general spatial or partial-trip modeling.

At the actual camp milestone, the story pauses with its current people, stock, skills, reservations and unfinished jobs. Continue changes only the chapter record. Household kits are ready before dispatch and sent after the actual ferry event. Early completion requires all needs covered and a dispatched ferry; it does not invent delivery at entry. Rain can close with unmet needs. Return to camp preserves the same world, unfinished work and settled allocations, rather than restarting or supplying a normalized opening.

The prototype does not yet simulate the provisioned nights or a further household scene. The retained outcomes make continuation possible; their next downstream use remains a real next design task. No new quota is claimed as story content.

## Keeping a world

Five independent story slots use the dedicated `human-camp-story-slots-v1` key. A new story or confirmed import creates a separate slot. Selecting another pauses the current story; removing an inactive slot keeps the active in-memory world and Save context. Removal has an explicit confirmation, and the other slots remain exact. Original experimental-game keys are never overwritten.

Download exports an individual story. File import first validates and previews without replacing the current story; confirmation adds a slot. Valid old Common Ground and Before-the-rain files use explicit migration. A busy old camp retains actual paid gathering and its original fixed output; inherited rest converts to availability without undoing recovery. Existing Rain entry, allocations, departure and ending remain fixed. New story research-control saves retain their actual settings, show a visible label and expose the active-idle recovery option when required.

Invalid imports leave the book unchanged. A corrupt stored book remains untouched with a raw-backup download; temporary new work is explicitly unsaved. Provider quota failures preserve the current in-memory camp and display a persistent download warning. Hidden file input focus is visibly outlined through its label.

The [pure slot adapter](camp-slots-contract.md) validates external books and every inactive save. Deeply frozen trusted books avoid repeated replay during ordinary updates: measured update-plus-serialization medians fell from roughly 110–1,880 ms to 0.9–3.9 ms, with all twelve output byte sequences unchanged. Cold five-slot/500-command loading still costs roughly 627 ms on the recorded machine; this is not a phone performance claim.

## Verification and boundaries

[Nine complete local browser groups](../artifacts/camp-ui/final/result.json) cover three viewport sizes, keyboard tabs, paid concurrent work, Stop/resume, export/import and reload, busy legacy entry, actual ferry/rain with a partial meal, Return, five-slot isolation, invalid/corrupt/quota handling, research presets and the eleven-entry chooser. The first two harness failures are retained: one assumed Save remained selected after an inactive removal (the UI was improved to keep that context), and another omitted an explicit Work-tab selection in the new research-control check. They are not presented as hidden host failures.

An [independent UI review](../artifacts/camp-review/ui/REVIEW.md) exercised an actual full campaign, twenty-one phase/viewport layouts, save flows, handovers and pause behavior. It caught disappearing-control focus, invisible file-input focus and a checkpoint heading hidden by retained scroll. Corrective checks bind the final source hashes. Default 375×667, 390×844 and 1280×800 layouts retain the HUD/dock without document scrolling; long detail panels scroll internally and short/high-zoom viewports use a readable fallback. Actual screen-reader announcements, Safari, physical mobile devices and genuine background-tab behavior are not certified by these headless checks; visibility was also tested with an explicitly synthetic event.

The source engine is independent of browser and LLM execution. The UI imports public hosts only. The bounded window journal supports a [specific integrity contract with measured costs](camp-validator.md), not authentication or a complete historical replay of pre-entry/post-Return life. Human explanation, measured authoring benefit, physiological calibration and qualified theological review remain open.
