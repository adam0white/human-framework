# Independent design review: the next workshop milestone

Date: 2026-09-07. Reviewed `src/games/workshop.js`, `src/human/index.js`, `src/core/model.js`, `web/workshop.js`, `tests/workshop-game.test.js`, `docs/workshop-integration.md`, `docs/roadmap.md`, and the project workflow. This memo proposes a playable experiment; it does not report an implemented feature or a new playtest.

## Recommendation

Build **one shift with three different seal-pump jobs**, one worker, one scarce replacement seal, shared fatigue, hunger and repair practice, and an end-of-shift account of the pumps actually running. Make the jobs visible from the beginning so the player can choose their order and decide where to spend the spare. Retain Before departure as the short introduction. Retain laboratory Solo Repair unchanged as the single-person control.

The user's feedback supports longer strategy and useful consequences of experience. It does not require a new cognitive faculty, an XP economy, several skill trees, or a campaign. Ordinary scheduling, route planning, scarce parts, visible job differences and partial success can test the requested improvement with the existing human component.

Three copies of the current pump will lengthen the click sequence without necessarily making the decisions better. At least one shared constraint must connect the jobs: the spare can be installed only once, time spent on an early attempt reduces the later options, and the worker carries condition and practice forward. The design succeeds if the player can explain a choice such as “I used the spare on the difficult intake, then recovered before attempting the remaining pump.” It need not make an elaborate controller outperform a simple rule.

## What the existing game already tells us

The recorded seeds 101–200 benchmark has 99/100 wins for the task-aware replacement route and 100/100 for each patch controller. No controller used a meal in those 300 runs. The patch routes finished faster on average; the more elaborate controller did not improve completion frequency. These results support the user's account that the introduction is forgiving. They do not support adding penalties to make the task-aware route win.

The current game already preserves practice from elapsed failed and interrupted work. `advanceAttempt` changes the skill before the host resolves success; `finishAttempt` does not grant a success or failure XP bonus. That is a useful existing contract. The immediate aftermath of a failed attempt can nevertheless have worse repair odds because effort and hunger accompany learning. With the existing coefficients, 35 minutes from skill 0.55 adds about 0.075 skill, while a patch adds 0.2425 fatigue and 0.07 hunger before any recovery. “You learned, therefore your next attempt is easier” would overstate what the model predicts.

Learning is currently reported in the failure explanation, but the skill estimate lives in researcher details. A longer shift needs to show the practical effect in normal play: skill change after work, current estimated chance, and a concise explanation that fatigue may offset practice. Show estimated probabilities as estimates, not a forecast of the next seeded draw.

## Design comparison

| Candidate | Why it could work | Main failure mode | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| One multi-pump shift | Jobs compete for time and one part; order has consequences; practice carries into another target; a failed early job leaves alternatives. | Repeated identical repairs become busywork; one obvious value-per-minute ordering or spare allocation solves every run. | Best next milestone. Bound the host to three distinct job profiles and test order/resource choices. |
| Two or three linked jobs across days | Strong sense of progression; recovery can separate practice gains from fatigue; an earlier failure can matter in a later job. | Overnight body reset and persistent skill create campaign balance and XP-farming questions; players may lose progression to a misunderstood reset; scripted job order limits strategy. | A credible later extension if one shift earns replay interest. Do not introduce persistent character progression now. |
| One pump with several faults or stages | Deeper diagnosis and repair sequence; fewer locations; a stable machine provides a legible objective. | Mandatory inspect/repair/test chains disguise a fixed checklist as strategy; unrelated fault types tempt unjustified skill transfer; random setbacks can become repetitive retry work. | Simpler rival if three jobs prove visually crowded. Require optional routes or competing stages; avoid making every stage mandatory. |

An endless job queue is a weaker alternate for this milestone. It gives repeated work and a high score but removes the reason to plan around a finite endpoint, amplifies grinding, and makes it harder to distinguish a good choice from simply playing longer.

## Concrete minimum scope

1. **One visible job board and three persistent targets.** A small auxiliary pump has a short, easier seal repair; the main intake is longer and more difficult but restores more stated service; a middle job offers a different time/effort compromise. These are varieties of the same repair skill, not silent transfer from seal fitting to electrical diagnosis. Give the pumps names, distinct appearances, public durations and meaningful differences in repair estimates. A change in color or hidden random condition alone does not count as a different job.
2. **A single finite shift.** Start by testing the existing four-hour budget with increased workload before extending it. Increase the duration only if the prototype cannot offer a forgiving partial result and a challenging full restoration. Choose the final duration from measured routes and play, not from a desired controller ranking. Suggested play target: roughly 10–15 consequential choices, excluding start/finish bookkeeping, with at least one recovery or resource decision after an early repair.
3. **One shared wrench, one replacement seal, finite meals.** A successful replacement consumes the spare; a failed fitting retains it, matching the existing clear rule. Patching remains available without a part. Preserve travel and storage ownership; keep the map compact enough that the player can forecast a return trip. Avoid inventory weight, cash, shopping and additional consumable types in this milestone.
4. **An order decision and a route decision.** The player chooses which pump to attempt, whether to patch or use the scarce replacement, whether to recover, and whether to retry or move to another job after failure. All three jobs are reachable at the start; do not hide the interesting choice behind a linear unlock chain.
5. **Partial success that stays earned.** Running pumps remain running. At the deadline, show exactly which pumps work and which still need repair or testing. If service values are used, display them before decisions and credit them only for verified flow. Prefer “2 of 3 pumps running; 6 of 9 service units restored” to an opaque composite score. A visible full-restoration objective supplies the optional challenge without turning a useful partial result into nothing.
6. **Experience within the shift.** Carry the existing repair skill forward. Credit actual practice minutes once, equally for success and failure. Let body and practice both affect estimates. Show the before/after skill change without claiming that failure itself was rewarded. No permanent XP, level-ups, cross-run accumulation or unlocks in this milestone.
7. **Keep the technical boundary frozen.** Host code owns target IDs, repairs, parts, travel, score and time; the existing public human API owns the same body and practice. A new host/save version is appropriate for incompatible multi-target state. Preserve the historical workshop and laboratory replay contracts. Do not claim broad framework portability from this second workshop variant.

Do not make meals mandatory simply to justify their existence. Starting hunger is 0.15 and increases by 0.002 per minute: even a 360-minute shift ends at 0.87 without food, below the hunger capacity ceiling for actions completed on time. A meal can still improve odds earlier; whether it earns its travel/time cost is an empirical question about these authored rules. Fatigue is likely to become relevant earlier. If meals remain rarely useful, report that result.

## Incentives and failure traps

**XP farming.** Never award bonus points for failures, interruptions, attempt counts, skill growth or unused meals. Repair practice should improve later ability, not be the objective. No work on an already running target. Splitting an attempt into smaller time advances must not increase skill beyond the same elapsed work. Zero-time canceled attempts must grant no practice. If repeatedly interrupting a favorable repair becomes the best way to train, compare it with completing useful work; do not solve the symptom with a secret punishment. An explicit practice action could be considered later if training itself becomes a desired choice.

**Random-number manipulation.** The current repair draw includes the global person attempt ID. Taking or canceling an unrelated action can therefore change the next repair draw. In a longer game this also confounds comparisons between “rest first” and “repair first.” For the new host, prefer a target-specific count of fully resolved repair attempts, with draws keyed by seed, target and that count. Unrelated rest, travel, inspection, UI rendering and zero-time interruptions should not select a different draw for the corresponding repair attempt. Keep hidden draws outside the public view. Version this behavior change.

**Repeated work.** A failed repair should open a legible decision: retry with the remaining time, replace if the spare remains, recover, or pursue another pump. If the same repair button is the only sensible action after every failure, three pumps have not delivered strategy. Do not introduce random destruction of completed pumps merely to extend play.

**Dead ends.** Preserve a patch route when the spare is spent; never allow an interrupted install to consume a part without its completed world effect. Display repair plus test time together so a player can see that a repair alone will not count. If no remaining repair-and-test route fits the clock, offer “End shift” and preserve earned service instead of forcing idle clicks. A visible warning is preferable to silently banning a risky attempt.

**Scoring.** A point for every repaired pump favors the shortest target; weighted values can instead force one obvious highest-value target. Both are ordinary design choices, but author them openly and inspect their consequences. Keep practice, morale and estimated future potential out of the final objective. Avoid a time bonus that makes additional useful service worth less than leaving early. Report service first and elapsed time second rather than combining them with arbitrary hidden weights.

**Inspection.** The current paid inspection reveals a small condition adjustment. A tiny estimate change may never justify ten minutes. In the new game, either give inspection information that can plausibly change the part/order decision or accept it as an optional weak action and report that. Do not require inspection as a mandatory tax or claim an information advantage when the rational decision remains the same.

**Legibility.** At each decision the player should see the running-job count, time remaining, current location, carried spare, target repair/test cost, and current effort/chance estimate. Keep broader limitations in collapsed model notes. Controller hints should stay hidden during playability checks. An action should not require multiple routine clicks merely to create a longer experience; keep partial-time controls available for interruption/save demonstrations without counting those clicks as strategic depth.

## Rejection tests and review gates

These are checks of an authored game, not claims about human behavior.

- **Strategy variety:** Compare fixed easy-first patching, high-service-first patching, a fixed spare-on-hardest route, and a simple recovery-aware route under identical public information. Sweep the three job orders and feasible spare allocations. Preserve all negative results. If one fixed recipe weakly dominates the alternatives across normal conditions and there are no intelligible circumstances favoring another choice, revise the job costs/values or simplify the promise of strategy. The richest controller winning is not an acceptance criterion.
- **Ordinary and pressure play:** A first ordinary failed repair must not mechanically eliminate every chance of useful service. There must be seeds and visible states where retrying remains feasible, and others where moving to a different job is reasonable. Full restoration can be harder than partial restoration. Report completion counts, service restored, elapsed time and failed attempts separately; do not report only wins or only successful-run averages.
- **Experience causality:** From equal starting state, compare equal-duration completed successful and failed practice receipts; skill credit must match. Then hold later body, action and outcome quantile fixed and compare a retest with practice active versus frozen. This distinguishes skill benefit from extra rest or a different random draw. If a normal early attempt produces no visible useful difference later, show that limitation rather than calling it progression.
- **No free farming:** Zero-time start/interrupt cycles leave skill and the corresponding repair draw unchanged. Splitting equal work duration does not amplify practice. Replaying a completed outcome cannot grant more practice, service or another part debit. Repaired/running targets cannot be used as unlimited work opportunities.
- **World and deadline integrity:** Only successful completed replacement consumes a spare. Testing is required for service credit. Travel and recovery consume time. Deadline interrupts incomplete effects without erasing prior service. Pending saves resume identically, including the current target, part ownership and repair-attempt counter. An exact-boundary completed test counts consistently.
- **Simple host integration:** New target interactions require no workshop-specific branches or duplicated body/skill state in the human component. Social switches leave laboratory Solo Repair behavior and outcome hashes unchanged. No peers, trust, promises, consent state, or social scoring enter this shift.
- **Human explanation:** With controller hints hidden, the player can explain the objective, why two pumps differ, where the spare went, and what an early failure changed. Keep the existing five-person/four-explanations formative gate as a future check; this independent review is not that playtest. Ask whether the longer run contains more meaningful choices, not simply whether it takes longer.

## Strong counterarguments

The best argument against this recommendation is that it risks turning a clear miniature into a spreadsheet of three nearly identical machines. If the board becomes busy and every route is apparent after one play, the one-pump/multiple-fault alternative may provide better narrative continuity and less navigation. Keep the introduction available and avoid adding a complex map merely because there are more targets.

A short linked-job sequence could communicate “experience matters later” more clearly than a shift because recovery between jobs separates learning from exhaustion. That is a real advantage. It is deferred because the current question is first whether experience and resource choices can improve one bounded session. If the shift's fatigue always obscures learning, a second job with an explicit recovery interval may be the simpler next experiment than changing practice coefficients.

Finally, a solvable scheduling game is not automatically a human simulation failure. An ordinary fixed heuristic may be the right controller for a small finite workshop. The player benefit can lie in understanding and choosing the tradeoff themselves. Broader architecture should be added only when a concrete player or host-authoring need survives comparison with that simpler solution.
