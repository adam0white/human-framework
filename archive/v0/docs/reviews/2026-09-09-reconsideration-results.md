## Strong existing control and metadata

After the manual arm completes its two extra `release` and `request` commands, its subsequent operation list matches the candidate's. Across **608 aligned post-intervention states**, the physical projection is exactly equal. The projection removes only host version, recent notices, last-response metadata, and commitment acceptance time/reason; it retains all other state, including commitment status/project/finish time, clock, people, paid accounts, assignments, contributions, materials and receipts.

Those excluded differences are real. The manual intervention withdraws and renews the commitment, resetting acceptance time to the cancellation minute; the automatic rule retains acceptance at 48. Their response and explanatory notices differ. Therefore the full saved states are not identical, and this result is not equivalence of every agreement/history fact. It does show that this automatic rule adds no physical outcome beyond the strong existing control in these four cases. Its two-command saving is an API interaction reduction, not a measured usability, attention or real-time benefit.

## Scope of the decision

Close this policy direction with its source, tests, positive frame findings and adverse roof findings intact. The unchanged policy already provides useful output and earlier roofs in the positive-paid cases; the existing explicit control can obtain the earlier frame when the player wants that tradeoff. The current candidate makes that tradeoff automatically without demonstrating an additional completed-project benefit over the available control.

This conclusion does not prove that finishing every trip is universally best, that reconsideration is never useful, or that the two commands have no human cost. The four selected timings, known developmental R0, fixed garden/food continuation, baseline-produced prefixes and common observation horizon limit the claim. No paid-time cutoff was tuned after seeing results, no new cases were added, and no public or automation change follows from this review.
