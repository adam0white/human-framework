## Uncertainties and limits

- D05 uses a radio-dial comparison for a short break at 594.518 s. That is indirect evidence. Its first literal stopping statement is 935.098 s. The earlier “I nearly tapped away” at 329.981 s is not sufficient to establish session departure.
- D14 states a conditional wish at 261.869 s. It is still an expressed wish, but does not assert immediate departure.
- D17 and D19 contain later self-reports that an earlier wish passed or partly passed. Their earlier actual words do not clearly express that wish. I do not turn frustration, pause-seeking, “Staying,” or a later reference into a measured earlier wish. D17: possible inferred precursor at 693.168 s; first clear current wish at 799.451 s. D19: unlocated earlier wish self-reported at 257.246 s; first clear current wish at 909.376 s.
- D25 supplies post-stop recollection rather than a live diary. Its 10.322 s reference identifies an observation, not measured wish onset or reading time. The table therefore leaves its measured wish time empty.
- D27’s 674.772 s reluctance is weaker than a current leave wish. The first clear leave wish is 688.745 s. D24’s earlier “brought me back in” at 720.337 s does not prove an earlier wish to leave.
- Wishes to take a break, stop for tonight, stop after the day, close the tab, or step away while leaving it running are preserved separately. Continued activity under an allotted time or assignment is not evidence that a wish passed. Positive reactions alone establish renewed interest, not necessarily removal of the wish.
- This coding cannot establish unspoken wishes, enjoyment from silence, voluntary retention, or reasons beyond the diary words. End-of-record evidence gives no later trajectory. A stated return intention is not an observed return.
- All 29 diaries are included. No exclusions were applied; the coordinator handles exclusions after freezing this coding.
