## Findings supported by frozen source inspection

- The built-in options accept only `full` and `baseline`; `step` calls `rankActions` directly. A general controller interface is absent (`src/core/model.js:93`, `src/core/simulation.js:103`). A manually supplied command can still override one actor, so the broader claim that no third controller can drive any actor would be too strong.
- Work scores directly multiply physical output while recovery and promise terms do not (`src/core/policy.js:19–42`). Equivalent changes of resource units can change priorities. The appropriate transformation must include all physical quantities that express the same resource, including consumption and initial progress when present, not only output and target.
- The recovery probe drives the first actor and leaves partners under Full (`src/experiments.js:15–27`). It establishes a feasible observable strategy, not an all-actor controller comparison.
- Capacity failure can cause synthetic eating without an eating request, and help chooses its own recipient (`src/core/simulation.js:79`, `109–116`). Those are concrete boundaries to reconsider for a host-owned world with inventory, targeting or commitments.
- Full is a reactive weighted scorer, not a multistep planner (`src/core/policy.js:9–46`). The deadline exploration contains counterexamples to any general superiority claim. Keeping multiple success, behavior and authoring criteria is more informative than requiring Full to win.
- The current state retains complete history, and `getView` flattens it before selecting recent events (`src/core/observation.js:19`). Bounded active state remains an integration requirement rather than a demonstrated capability.

## A targeted execution check of the social claim

After reading review B, Codex ran the unchanged frozen 0.2 snapshot with Full for seeds 101–200 in all four shipped presets: 400 simulations. This was a follow-up verification by Codex; Claude did not run these tests. The check counted actual `help` executions, final kept/expired promises, and directed trust values different from the initial `0.5`.

| Preset | Runs | Help executions | Promises kept | Promises expired | Directed trust values changed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Courier | 100 | 0 | 200 | 0 | 200 |
| Repair | 100 | 0 | 200 | 0 | 200 |
| Commons | 100 | 0 | 100 | 100 | 200 |
| Solo | 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

In Commons, Idris kept his promise in all 100 runs and Meryem's help promise expired in all 100. The narrow finding is that Full did not automatically select assistance in these conditions, and the relationship coupling did not produce an objective advantage in the recorded benchmark. Trust and promise records did change. This does not show that manual assistance has no effect, that the entire social mechanism never executes, or that every possible scenario would behave identically. A negative result is a reason to design a discriminating social experiment, not sufficient evidence by itself to delete a supported action.

## Corrections and recommendations that should not be adopted automatically

1. **Review A's module count is inconsistent.** Its prose says two modules are inert in every shipped world, while its own table shows only relationships inactive across all four; beliefs and commitments affect at least one preset. Moreover, equality on recorded aggregate metrics alone cannot establish equality of every latent state or decision. The targeted execution above supports the narrower assistance claim.
2. **Observation of promises is an explicit scenario assumption.** Review A says trust updates reach peers who observed nothing. The model reference explicitly declares co-located actors and public announced promises/acts (`docs/model-reference.md:112`). The implementation has no general witness or event-access model, which matters for future hosts, but its behavior is consistent with the declared current scope.
3. **Fast learning is an authored proxy, not proof of a specific psychological process.** The shipped coefficients produce rapid within-shift proficiency gains and there is no runtime forgetting. That makes calibration and construct labeling worth examining. It does not establish that the variable must be warm-up or familiarity, nor that a simple task cannot be learned within a shift. Renaming it would require a defined construct and evidence, rather than merely a reviewer analogy.
4. **Weighted reasons are not necessarily instrumental reasons.** Both reviewers infer that a promise represented by a utility term must be instrumental or that noninstrumental reasons are absent. An additive representation imposes compensability; it does not by itself show that every valued end is a means to production. The implemented promise term is added independently of output. Whether compensability is appropriate for a particular obligation is a separate design and interpretive question. Their critiques usefully motivate comparing threshold, constraint-based and weighted policies; they do not prove a universal replacement is required.
5. **Positive religious representation is an open design question.** A's school-specific theological and legal suggestions are explicitly recollection, were not externally verified, and should not become runtime rules or public religious claims from this review. The distinction between preserved intention text and an actual model of intention is useful. A proposed worship or fasting scenario requires separate source research, scoped design and qualified review. The current absence of such a scenario does not invalidate the source method or justify deleting the project's broader grounding.
6. **Several proposed gates are arbitrary or reuse examined evidence.** B's roughly 200-line abstraction cutoff is not a validated measure of usefulness. Its proposed evaluation seeds 1001–1100 have already been examined in the recovery exploration. Reserve new conditions and seeds before tuning when a prospective evaluation is intended. A's requirement that Full win completion comparisons conflicts with its own argument that obligations and player-visible distinctions may have value without increasing production.
7. **Existing commitments still govern delivery.** B's proposal to remove the public historical replay engine conflicts with preserving historical replay access. Both proposals to cut existing presets or actions need actual product evidence and scope judgment. The single-person Solo control should remain unchanged; new social or religious experiments belong alongside it rather than silently altering its interpretation.

The reviews agree most strongly on retaining the observation/request/execution/replay distinctions, separating the world from human-state responsibilities, giving external controllers an explicit boundary, using serious simpler competitors, and treating scientific, theological, authoring and gameplay claims as different questions. Those are the parts most directly supported by the inspected implementation. No external source or theological claim was independently verified during this follow-up.
