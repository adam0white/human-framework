# Game lab: ideas (2026-10-19)

The owner asked for ideas (2026-10-19): "I'm sure we need other things and experiments and background processes etc to turn this into a game lab. Let's ideate on those too."
These are proposals. None of them has been started. "Pick" marks the recommended first steps.

## What the lab already has

- HF releases on npm, with provenance.
- 8 games on subdomains, plus a collection page.
- An HF-character playtester harness, now being expanded with edge-pushers, cohorts and build-to-build comparison.
- Source-unread LLM playtesters, with their words recorded verbatim.
- An hourly heartbeat that pushes stalled builders.

## Ideas

| # | Idea | What it does | Value | Cost | Pick |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nightly cohort run** | A scheduled job runs the HF playtester cohort against every live game's pushed tip and writes one dated report per game: boredom, quit points, edges, and the change from the last build. | High: regressions in fun show up without anyone asking | Low, once the harness has cohorts | Pick |
| 2 | **Lab dashboard** | One page at human.adamwhite.work/lab/ shows each game's HF pin, live commit, last cohort scores, open HF requests and the latest playtester quote. | High: one place to see the whole lab | Low | Pick |
| 3 | **Real-player telemetry, opt-in** | Games already export a playtest replay. Add a "send this run to the notebook" button that posts it to an R2 bucket, so the owner's and friends' runs feed the same reports as the simulated ones. | Highest: real players are the strongest signal | Medium; needs an owner decision on storage and privacy | Pick, after the owner says yes |
| 4 | **Sim-vs-human calibration** | Compare the owner's real runs (from #3) with HF playtester runs on the same seed, to find where the simulated players are wrong. | High: this keeps the recursive loop honest | Medium | Pick, after #3 |
| 5 | **HF request pipeline** | Every game's `hf-requests.md` is collected into one queue, with duplicates merged and each request voted on by how many games want it. That queue sets the next HF release. | Medium: today this is a hand-maintained append log | Low | Pick |
| 6 | **Weekly game jam** | Each week, one builder agent makes a one-day prototype from a prompt (a genre plus an HF faculty). The cohort and two LLM testers score it, and the owner picks whether it graduates to a full game. | High fun and discovery; feeds the collection | Medium tokens | Yes |
| 7 | **Faculty probes** | Tiny toy scenes, one per HF faculty (promptings, forecast, muhasaba, voices), that show the faculty working in isolation. Each scene doubles as a regression test and a design sketchbook. | Medium | Low | Yes |
| 8 | **Recursive improvement loop** | The designer persona proposes tuning, a builder applies it on a branch, and the cohort compares the two builds. The result is kept only if the cohort and a human (or LLM reviewer) agree. | Medium; it stays honest only with #4 | Medium | Later |
| 9 | **HF dev-team characters** | Builders and reviewers get HF personalities (a stubborn designer, an anxious QA). | Low: flavour, and adds noise to the signal | Low | No, unless just for fun |
| 10 | **Taste model** | Collect every owner verdict ("still not for me", "I need more!") with what each game does. Before a new concept is built, it is checked against the owner's known taste. | Medium: saves builds the owner won't like | Low | Yes |
| 11 | **Cross-game people** | One HF person (Selim from The Pass, say) can be exported from one game and imported into another, carrying their memories with them. | High wow; a stress test for HF state portability | Medium | Experiment |
| 12 | **Spend and token ledger** | Per-game token and Workers AI spend, recorded weekly. | Medium: lets the lab keep a budget | Low | Yes |
| 13 | **Public lab notebook** | Each week, a short post on what was learned: a negative finding, a playtester quote, an HF change. | Medium: gives the lab an outside audience | Low | Optional |

## "Too much ouroboros?" (opinion)

No, as long as two conditions hold:

1. Every loop ends in real player traces, or in an owner or human acceptance.
2. Simulated playtesters are calibrated against real runs (#4).

Without those, the lab tunes games toward what HF-people like, and HF-people are built by the same team. That is a closed loop. HF game designers are worth one experiment (in progress). HF dev-team characters are flavour and should not be infrastructure.

## Suggested order

1. #2 dashboard and #5 request pipeline: cheap, and useful today.
2. #1 nightly cohort run, once the harness expansion lands.
3. #3 telemetry, after an owner decision; then #4 calibration.
4. #6 weekly jam and #10 taste model.
