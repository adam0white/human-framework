Preregistered on 2026-09-08 before execution of the new host/receiver outcomes. Status: input and policy design pending root's source-freeze commit; no new evaluation has run in this lane. The completed private Across the Cut study remains historical evidence and does not certify the new receiver.

## Exact scope and fixed choices

[Inputs](../artifacts/across-player/inputs.json) specify six authored worlds and eight controls, for 48 policy records. Four further interventions share P3: keeper inspect 0–1, repair 1–7 and release at 7, then stop or continue at the receiver report actually received at 8, each with uninterrupted and actual-receipt save/resume variants. These are prescribed software cases, not independent samples, withheld tests, or human-usefulness evidence. No broad historical matrix is rerun.

All current arms use the new host's automatic one-minute available recovery while unassigned, counted in `paid.availableRecovery`. The old private host instead charged active idle maintenance. Current arms share the new rule; no old-to-new physical-equivalence or causal recovery claim follows.

The five shared-receiver controls keep the delivered receiver policy fixed: fixed-early keeper (release no earlier than 7), fixed-conservative (13), cart-first keeper timing (9), paid contact, and source-time adaptive radio. Calling their fixed keepers "no-radio" would hide the reporting receiver's costs. They are therefore labeled shared-receiver controls and all receiver radio/attendance/work are counted.

Three separately labeled **joint no-radio** controls change both policies: cart-first early (keeper release floor 9), cart-first conservative (13), and immediate cart with an inactive keeper. The first two receivers inspect and repair where useful; in a short-inlet early-launch world they take the cart after repair, return by 13 and attend through 15. In late-launch worlds they attend during (13,18], then take cart insurance when useful. A known impossible inlet triggers immediate useful cart. The two keeper thresholds expose the tradeoff between an unknown early short-inlet deadline and an unknown late long-inlet readiness; neither receives hidden setup. These are substantial joint policy differences, not matched receiver comparisons.

The paid-contact keeper inspects its own valve. If short, it pays travel to dock, inspects there, pays a contact transmission if the peer is present, returns and uses known facts to avoid a hopeless release. If its own valve is long, it skips the slow contact trip and uses the conservative fixed threshold. This fallback is declared in advance. Contact outcomes include all travel, inspection and transmission costs.

The adaptive keeper sends one initial first-hop factual report, then repairs in one-minute decisions. Its inlet-attendance lower bound uses latest source-time requirement/progress plus known inspection and completed receiver transmission intervals, never the latest arrival as current truth or an assumed player schedule. The bound is optimistic about all remaining unobserved receiver work. It can delay release, stop an unfinished release whose arrival is now proven too early/late, or preserve water when the launch cannot be met. Silence is not a refusal. Exact policy code is [frozen alongside the inputs](../artifacts/across-player/policies.mjs).

P1 is the predeclared communication-overhead hypothesis: radio may retain service but pay extra transmissions and delay arrival relative to simpler controls. Record the actual outcome even if this prediction fails. P2 tests delay-six evidence during the long-inlet preparation. P3 is the required early long-inlet receipt case. P4 tests early long-keeper cart insurance. P5 has both repairs long and a late launch. P6 loses all reports. Channel overrides cover both senders' completion slots 1–30 and do not depend on observed policy traffic.
