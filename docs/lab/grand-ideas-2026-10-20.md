# Grand ideas (2026-10-20)

The owner, 2026-10-20: "these games are becoming real fun. I need more of everything, more games and more grandiose ideas."
These are proposals from the lab's lead designer. "Pick" marks the ones built. Companion to [the game-lab ideas](../game-lab-2026-10-19.md).

## What the pick has to pass

Taste evidence is from [game-taste-2026-10-19.md](../game-taste-2026-10-19.md) and the [ledger](feedback-ledger.md). Confirmed: the owner likes simulation built up from groups of people, hidden causes with no visible dice, delayed feedback on his own decisions, and "a classic with a twist of better simulated human beings". He rejected The Rope (long, silent, low-agency). He avoids multiplayer and online games "as they're too addictive for me". Assumed: an ant-farm pull comes from watching many small agents carry something you started.

Hard limits: games are static assets (no server, database or Durable Objects; only lab telemetry is lifted). No game code in `packages/human`. Lessons L7 (fill the first hour), L15 (week one bites) and L19 (no named problem without a verb) apply to every concept.

## Why Hearsay first

- It is the most psychology-first concept and needs no new HF faculty: beliefs, source trust, impressions by testimony, `retell` and norms exist in 2.9.0.
- It has the ant-farm pull in a literal form: a trail spreads through many small agents from one act of yours.
- The naughty edge is implied, not stated: the game is an innocent neighbourhood, and gossip is a choice the player discovers.
- It keeps faith quiet: some neighbours hold that talebearing is wrong and will not repeat it, in their own words. That is one norm among others, never the theme.
- L7 and L15: the first rumour is planted in the first minute and reaches its first ear within a few game hours. L19: every problem the town shows (a cooled friendship, a cancelled order) has a verb: speak to someone, deny, apologise, or plant a counter-story.
- It is static, cheap enough to build in this session, and leaves The Agency (#11) and A Thousand Years (#4) as the next candidates.

## Why The Agency second (Good Hands)

Built 2026-10-20 as **Good Hands** (`~/code/goodhands`, https://goodhands.adamwhite.work). You run a one-room placement agency in Izmir for one November. Candidates are HF people whose true traits differ from how they present; Nermin learns them from what they say (`selfReport`), what others say (`hear`, weighted by her trust in the speaker) and what she sees (`observeAct`), and vouches for them to families. Placements keep living: seeded household moments test true traits, and word of mouth on Fridays and Saturdays (`converse`) brings or loses families and candidates.

- It is the owner's named love (scouting, as in football management) with people, and it is psychology-first: the gap between presentation and character is the whole game.
- It has many verbs (interview, reference, trial, place, check in), which answers the recurring "less watching" quits (F-227, F-229).
- The naughty edge is implied: placed workers confide what goes on inside the houses, and families ask what Nermin hears. The game never names it.
- The grandiose candidates (#12, #4) are rated High and would not fit one session that also deploys and runs up to four test rounds. They stay next. Good Hands gets its scale from a city of households and a stream of candidates instead.

## Adversarial review (Codex, 2026-10-20) and what changed

Full text: `scratch/grand-ideas-review.md` (local). Its findings, checked against source, and the response:

1. **`retell` does not distort, and its strength halves to nothing in two hops.** Confirmed in `memory/memory.ts`. Hearsay now spreads claims with `converse`, which already carries reputation claims belief by belief at the speaker's credence, tags unfavourable claims about an absent person with the held 'backbiting' norm, and lets low-honesty speakers lie when the host offers a temptation. The bend at each mouth is host-authored and bounded: a talkative, low-honesty teller may sharpen a claim one step along a fixed ladder, by a seeded hash, and the game says who sharpened it only in the replay.
2. **The opening promises movement, not a game.** Accepted. The player now has a stake from minute one: you keep the tea garden by the fountain, and you live on being the person whose word is good. Your son hopes to marry the baker's daughter, whose father is wavering. On the first morning you overhear one true and damaging thing. Before minute ten there is one refusal (a neighbour will not repeat it), one changed greeting, and one choice about repair.
3. **The integration claim hid work.** Accepted. `hear` writes impressions and `converse` writes beliefs; the host owns the claim vocabulary (HF's reputation traits), who meets whom, and the consequences (custom at a shop, the engagement, greetings, confrontation). Apology is a state change: it costs your standing with the listener and can restore theirs with the target.
4. **A full trail kills the mystery.** Accepted. You see conversations you witness and what people tell you. The full route appears only at the end of the fortnight and in the replay.
5. **Boundaries.** Agreed; The Commons stays a no without an owner lift. Sleepers stays a maybe for the faith-as-theme risk.
6. **Portability and scale oversold.** Agreed; costs for #3 and #4 are higher than first written.
7. **Bolder missing option.** Added as #12, The Long Table.
