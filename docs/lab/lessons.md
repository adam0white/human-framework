# Lab lessons

Every game builder reads this before starting a game or a polish round. Each lesson is a rule the lab learned from feedback, in plain words. A lesson is here only if at least two ledger rows or an owner direction support it. The evidence lists the ledger ids in [feedback-ledger.md](feedback-ledger.md). To add a lesson, write it as the next `L<n>`, cite the rows, and route the rows to it with `lesson:L<n>`. Lessons are confirmed in the sense that the feedback is verbatim; the causes are the game builders' own readings unless a source says it was replayed.

### L1 — A refusal says why, in the person's voice.
A no, a deferral, or a yes that follows a no should name what decided it, in the speaker's words. Without a reason, players read the answer as dice or as a bug. With a reason, they trade against it and believe the person.
Evidence: F-014, F-015, F-026, F-041, F-053, F-075, F-080, F-086

### L2 — No stock line repeats within a night.
Give each person their own pool of lines and rest a line after it is used. HF's own stock words (assent, deferral, the evening lapse line) repeat across people, so a host should vary them or ask HF to.
Evidence: F-030, F-031, F-034, F-045, F-072

### L3 — Every remembered line must be true to what happened.
Check each memory line against a replay. Do not say "you let it go" after the player insisted, do not thank someone who already left, and do not put words in the margin the player did not choose.
Evidence: F-019, F-021, F-030, F-032, F-045, F-062, F-065, F-085

### L4 — Every player verb gets a visible answer.
A tap with no answer reads as a bug. A verb that only logs a line makes the player feel mute. Show a reply, even "nobody heard you", and show an order while it is carried out.
Evidence: F-011, F-039, F-058, F-072, F-077, F-082, F-084, F-085

### L5 — Show the numbers a decision needs, in plain words, before the player commits.
Say what a choice costs in time, what is due, and how near the goal is, in a sentence. This keeps "show, don't count" for inner states and still lets the player plan. Do not use bars for a person's inner state.
Evidence: F-020, F-044, F-069, F-070, F-081

### L6 — Slow the clock when a choice is waiting.
Fast speed must drop to slow for a knock at the door, a fall, a hunger call, a promised hour, or an open sheet. Players lose the moment that mattered when the clock runs on.
Evidence: F-043, F-074, F-083, F-091

### L7 — Fill the first hour and the quiet stretches.
Put a decision, a refusal or a small event inside the first minutes. When nothing is on screen, fast-forward or add small life. Long empty stretches (a winter, the small hours, an empty rail) are where players quit.
Evidence: F-007, F-008, F-025, F-051, F-069, F-073

### L8 — A save holds still, and resuming is obvious.
Nothing moves while the page is shut. The title page offers one clear way back to the game. Closing a panel must not look like quitting.
Evidence: F-040, F-071

### L9 — Phones first: the actions stay above the fold at 360x780.
Test every panel with two or more people present and with a long label. Buttons below the fold, a clipped menu or a shifting layout cost players real taps.
Evidence: F-093, F-094, F-095, F-096

### L10 — A free override becomes the strategy, so pushing needs a visible price.
If insisting, pressing or urging always works and costs nothing visible, players stop choosing. Show the cost where the player can see it (a mood, a remembered line, a later refusal) and let it grow with use.
Evidence: F-015, F-016, F-023, F-055, F-056, F-080, F-087, F-088

### L11 — Make a found edge playable and slightly rewarding, then measure it.
The owner asks that edges stay a bit fun and enter the game's own calculations rather than only be closed. When a tester finds a cheap or cruel path, give it an answer in the fiction and check that it does not win outright.
Evidence: F-003, F-023, F-056, F-057, F-078, F-079

### L12 — Hints are honest odds, and the player can learn the information gap.
A tag that looks like a chance must behave like one. A game that says "you caught none of them" must show how to catch something.
Evidence: F-037, F-042, F-052

### L13 — Tie big beats to the player's own acts, and let the player be there.
The moments players praised came from something they did earlier and came back (a kept promise, a let-go, a warning that was not believed). The moments they resented happened offscreen as one log line.
Evidence: F-006, F-009, F-018, F-022, F-028, F-047, F-059, F-063, F-064, F-074, F-076, F-092

### L14 — Close the threads the game opens, and end on one open question.
A hook that never pays off (a photo, a promise, a visit) reads as a dropped thread. End a session on one named question the player wants answered. This follows the owner's request to start from what brings players back.
Evidence: F-004, F-060, F-061

### L15 — Start with natural difficulty, and check that week one bites.
The owner prefers hard-where-natural, relaxed later. Check with attentive and inattentive play that the first stretch has stakes. Several games paid off in the first week without any input.
Evidence: F-001, F-017, F-036, F-054, F-078, F-086, F-089

### L16 — Read simulated testers as a different signal from people.
HF-character testers are good at finding loops, cliffs and fatigue. They were never bored in these games. Hard-to-please LLM testers and the owner were. Use all three, and trust the owner's play most.
Evidence: F-002, F-035, F-066, F-081

### L17 — Check a tester's report against a replay before changing the game.
Several reports were harness artefacts: a first ask that failed after "open", and tips the text harness left out. Replay the export, then fix what replays.
Evidence: F-024, F-027

### L18 — Run idle choice headlessly for loops and wandering before release.
People left alone drank and relieved every four minutes, stood three at the dish pit, walked home to drink, answered every ask with the same chore, or flipped yes and no for nine days. Each came from a scoring detail, not a story reason.
Evidence: F-012, F-029, F-046, F-090
