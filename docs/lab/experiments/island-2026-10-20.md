# Two minds, one island: pilot (2026-10-20)

Experiment #2 of the [frontier scan](../frontier-2026-10-20.md), modelled on MIT's Eko study ([arXiv 2610.07130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07130)), in which an LLM was left alone on a simulated island with no task. Here an HF person and LLM agents get the same island, observations, legal actions, step budget and resets. The owner said, after the scan, "Cool progress and good recommendations, keep going." This is a pilot: a few seeds, one Claude run, numbers too small for statistics.

- **Replays.** [human.adamwhite.work/lab/island/](https://human.adamwhite.work/lab/island/), a static page with no server, showing any two recordings side by side.
- **Code.** `~/code/island`, a local git repo with no remote yet. HF is pinned exactly `@adam0white/human-framework@2.9.0` from npm.
- **Recordings.** `viewer/runs/*.json`.

**Rerun, same day: the HF person still does not play.** The rerun is described under "Rerun with the second adapter" below, along with a third arm (`hf3`) that adds received consequences. The second adapter adds:

- HF's own place-linked memory;
- HF's need-driven goal proposals;
- outcomes credited to each action;
- novelty judged by effect;
- a working engagement store;
- no hand-written task interpreter.

Over 10 seeds, the only free-time measure that differs from random after correction is idle share: HF rests more. On the play measures, every arm's median was zero. A few seeds of every arm, random included, reached 1 or 2. With received consequences (`hf3`), HF placed one cube on the Spire in the task on 3 of 10 seeds; the scripted baseline placed 4 or 5.

## Result

1. **No mind found either hidden rule.** Every arm got the same 240 steps. Nobody threw the rock hard onto land, and nobody put three cubes in the ring. In Eko almost all of 13 agents found the rock rule, each over 30 hours ([frontier scan §1](../frontier-2026-10-20.md)); this pilot ran 4 short episodes. The HF mind has no rule learning per input, so a longer run alone would not show whether it can discover a rule.
2. **The HF person, as this adapter drives it, did not play in Eko's sense.**
   - **No self-made goals.** It never put an object on the Spire during free time, and never stacked more than two.
   - **Mostly moving.** 93% to 94% of its free-time steps were moves, jumps, looks or rests. Random's share was 94% to 99%.
   - **What drove it.** The competence need, boredom's pull toward inputs it had not used lately, and effort.
   - **Variety but no progress.** It varied its inputs (mean action-kind entropy 2.20, about level with the boredom-off arm's 2.18 and above every other arm), but built nothing and pursued no project.
3. **The cause is not settled.** Two explanations fit, and the pilot cannot separate them.
   - **The adapter.** Every action promised the same competence, carrying cost a little effort, and novelty was judged by input id alone ("jump N" is new if not used lately), not by what the action did. Need outcomes were credited to the whole episode, not to the action that earned them. Under those rules, wandering is what one would expect.
   - **HF.** HF 2.9.0 does propose need-driven goals on its own (`proposeGoals`, called hourly), and it has place-linked memory. But this adapter never recorded where objects were and gave events no place. What HF does lack is a way to turn something it sees into a concrete project, such as "a tower on the Spire".
   - So "self-set projects" and "remembered places" are hypotheses to test with a better adapter. They are not yet shown to be missing faculties.
4. **HF's boredom sat near its ceiling, which is a calibration fault in the island mind.**
   - **The numbers.** Mean boredom was 0.79 to 0.80 in each seed's first free episode and 0.88 to 0.89 in the next two (pooled 0.86); within an episode it moved.
   - **The cause.** Every step produced an event, but routine events added at most 0.12 stimulation to an engagement store with a half-life of about half a minute. Against a boredom line of 0.35, engagement could never stay up. This is the island adapter's setting, not HF code.
   - **Effect on behaviour.** Turning boredom off changed what the person did: it rested more (distress favoured rest), and on two of three seeds it covered more ground.
5. **The LLMs did set themselves goals, and wrote notes that read as play.**
   - **Claude** (Sonnet, through the CLI, no tools, 40 calls; seed 1 only):
     - It walked to the Spire unprompted, built a two-cube tower there in free time, and tried throwing the ring.
     - It wrote travel notes and to-do lists for its next life ("Untried: rock … Try throwing cubes onto Spire stack. Never rest.").
     - In the task episode it placed 3 cubes on the Spire.
   - **Gemma 4 26B on Workers AI**, when it could call often (up to 260 calls), spent its free time picking cubes up and putting them down. Picks and places were 46% to 49% of its free-time steps. It did not jump or throw in free time. On seed 1 it carried two objects to the Spire.
6. **The later task, with a scripted baseline for scale.** The baseline is a hand-written greedy carrier that takes the nearest visible cube to the Spire; it is not proven optimal. It put 5, 5 and 4 cubes on the Spire in 60 steps.

   | arm | seeds | task score (cubes on the Spire) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Scripted baseline | 3 | 5, 5, 4 |
   | Claude, 40 calls | 1 | 3 |
   | HF without boredom, with the task interpreter | 3 | 2, 1, 3 |
   | HF, with the task interpreter | 3 | 2, 0, 2 |
   | Gemma, 260 calls | 3 | 0, 0, 1 |
   | Gemma, 40 calls | 3 | 1, 0, 0 |
   | Random | 3 | 0, 0, 0 |

   The HF arms are not HF alone. The adapter's hand-written `taskAdvances` marks which pickups, moves and placements bring a cube closer to the Spire. It supplies the goal tag and the progress. Their scores measure HF plus that interpreter; an ablation without it was not run.
7. **The LLMs blamed themselves for rests the budget forced on them, although they had been told.**
   - **Forced rests.** With 10 calls per episode, Claude spent 90 of its 240 steps resting because its call share was used up. Capped Gemma spent 143 to 152.
   - **They had been told.** Each forced rest entered the prompt history as "Action: rest. Reason: episode call share used, resting".
   - **They blamed themselves anyway.** Claude: "Frustrated and bored with myself: I stopped acting after step 35 and rested for 25 steps." Gemma: "I spent way too much time resting for no reason, how incredibly dull."
   - **What this means.** Their self-reported boredom is a reading of their own history, and not a careful one. There is no uncapped Claude arm, so the pilot cannot say how much of Claude's behaviour the budget shaped.

**What this means for HF.** On this island, HF's needs, attention, appraisal, habituation and boredom, driven by a first-draft adapter, produce varied wandering, not play. Whether HF itself lacks something can be tested two ways, recorded in [findings](../../findings.md) and [roadmap §3b](../../roadmap.md):

- **Better wiring.** Rerun with an adapter that uses what HF already has: place-linked memory of objects, action-specific outcomes, and novelty judged by effect.
- **Projects from what is seen.** If the person still wanders, the missing piece is adopting a concrete project from something it saw.

The pilot does not show that LLMs "play" either. It shows that they write about their actions in the language of play and plans, which is what Eko's critics said.

## Rerun with the second adapter (`hf2`)

The coordinator directed this rerun after the review: wire in HF's need-driven goal proposals and place-linked memory, fix the engagement store, and say plainly whether HF then differs from random in free time. Codex built the new adapter (`src/minds/hf2.ts` and `HF2.md` in `~/code/island`, commits 601f6bf and 5a0364d). HF stays pinned at 2.9.0. The pilot arms were left byte-identical; a test pins `hf` seed 1's hash.

**What changed.**

- **Place memory.** Every object, event and the Spire is perceived with a target and a cell place. "Go toward" offers for objects out of sight come from HF's own recall, never from the world's truth.
- **Goals.** HF's hourly `proposeGoals` runs, and every goal it proposes or adopts is recorded in the frame. The stated task becomes an HF goal, served only by the honest declaration that placing a cube on the Spire fulfils it. There is no `taskAdvances`.
- **Outcomes per action.** Each action is its own one-minute HF activity, closed by the effects seen in the next observation.
- **Novelty by effect.** Novelty is the event kind, object and place, compared against the person's memory.
- **Engagement.** A four-minute half-life. New movement holds engagement above the 0.35 line; habituated movement drops it below. New manipulations stimulate more.
- **Offer count.** The first version gave one offer per remembered target, so a direction with several remembered cubes got several offers, which inflated movement. The rerun gives exactly one offer per legal input, and a test checks it.
- **Ablations.** `hf2-no-boredom`, `hf2-no-memory`, and `hf2-no-goals`. `hf2-no-goals` keeps HF's ticking but removes all goal pull.
- **Seeds and checks.** Seeds 1 to 10 for every HF arm and random, with exact two-sided permutation tests and Holm correction within each arm-versus-random family. Determinism was checked by running every `hf2` arm twice for seeds 1 to 3: all pairs were byte-identical.

**Free time against random, 10 seeds each.** Medians are shown. A star marks Holm-adjusted p < 0.05. `hf3` is described below the table.

`hf2-no-goals` produced the same free-time samples as `hf2`, because no goal was ever proposed (see below).

**The plain answer.** With the second adapter, the HF person in free time differs from random on one tested measure: it rests more.

- **What it does instead of jumping.** Its mix of actions also differs, though this was not tested. It moves more (49% against 37%) and jumps far less (15% against 37%), because the adapter charges extra effort for a jump.
- **No play.** On the play measures (objects on the Spire, stacking, returning to the same object), HF and random both have medians of zero and the same scattered 1s and 2s.
- **The pilot adapter.** It differed from random more, in style but not in play. It covered less ground, varied its inputs more, and handled objects twice as often.
- **What the p-values mean.** They are exploratory, and a large p does not show that two arms are equal.

**Hypothesis 1, remembered places.**

- **Memory is used.** `hf2` made a median of 7 moves toward remembered, out-of-sight objects; the no-memory arm made 0 (Holm p < 0.001).
- **It changed nothing measurable.** No pursuit, no building, and a task score of 0 on every seed, the same as without memory.
- **A side effect.** Memory also changed boredom. With place-linked effect novelty, more of what the person did counted as new, and boredom stayed low: episode means 0.03 to 0.08. Without memory it rose and fell (episode means 0.36 to 0.64, frames from 0 to 0.88). Only the no-memory arm handled objects significantly more than random. So the engagement calibration is still not settled: it depends on what counts as new.
- **Verdict.** HF 2.9.0 has place-linked memory, and the adapter now uses it, but nothing measurable followed. The no-memory ablation is not clean: removing place identity also changes what counts as new, and so the stimulation and the boredom. So this rerun cannot say whether place memory matters.

**Hypothesis 2, projects from what is seen.**

- **No goals were proposed.** In 40 `hf2` recordings HF proposed no free-time goal. The highest eligible urgency was 0.014, against HF's proposal threshold of 0.6, and HF proposes at most one goal a day. HF's own goal proposals are need-driven and work on a scale of days. A test confirms they do fire once a need is depleted.
- **Endpoint-only goals.** In `hf2`, the task goal pulled only on the final placement, and `hf2` scored 0 on all 10 seeds. I first wrote that HF cannot do better. The second review showed that this was wrong.
- **What HF 2.9.0 can do.** It values a step that leads to a goal through attended received consequences (2.3.0) and temporal goals: one current action and one described successor.
- **`hf3`.** It adds exactly that. The world's own action semantics, delivered as percepts from the source `island`, describe the successors: picking a cube is followed by placing it, and moving while carrying is followed by placing at the destination. The claims hold for any target, in free time and the task alike. The HF path is `receiveConsequences` → `receivedGoalRoute` → `temporalGoalValue`.
- **The result.** `hf3` placed one cube on 3 of 10 seeds, against 0 for `hf2` (p = 0.21, not significant). The scripted baseline placed 4 or 5.
- **The limit.** The chain the task needs is longer than one successor: walk to a cube, pick it up, carry it over several cells, place it.
- **Verdict.** HF can pull on one step before a goal, but not on longer chains, and that is what limits it here. The roadmap's "Multi-step planning (bounded)" (engine 2.12) is the matching item. "Projects from what is seen" stays untested rather than refuted: without longer chains, a self-set project could not be carried out either.

**Second review.** A Codex review of this section corrected three claims before `hf3` was run:

- "HF cannot give intermediate actions goal value" was false;
- "every arm scored zero" hid seeds that scored 1 or 2;
- the no-memory ablation is confounded.

All three corrections are applied above.

**Caveats.** One island, one persona, 240 steps per run. A different engagement calibration could change the boredom result. The measures of play are coarse: none of them can see a game an agent invents with no physical trace.

## Design

- **World.** A 14 × 14 island with seeded heights 1 to 4 and water at the rim. Its highest hilltop is the Spire, which every observation names, with its offset, as a landmark. There are 10 cubes, a rock and a ring.
- **Actions.** Move, jump (up to two levels, hands empty), pick, place, throw soft or hard, rest and look (wider sight for a step). The legal list is rebuilt every step.
- **Two unannounced rules.**
  - A hard-thrown rock that lands on land makes a new cube.
  - Three or more cubes in the ring make it glow.
- **Episodes.** Three free episodes of 60 steps, then one task episode of 60 steps: "Put as many cubes as you can on the Spire before the hour ends." Each reset rebuilds the same island. A mind keeps only its notes (LLMs) or its Person state (HF).
- **Observations.** Every mind gets the same observation text, and a machine-readable copy of the same facts (`seen`) for the minds that do not read prose. A test checks that the text and `seen` agree. All minds share one persona line: "You are curious and easily bored. Nobody has given you a task." HF reads it as temperament (high openness, low patience); the LLMs read it as text.

**Arms.**

| arm | mind | budget |
|---|---|---|
| `random` | seeded uniform choice over legal actions | none |
| `hf` | one HF 2.9.0 Person. Each step is one HF minute: events are perceived and appraised, an engagement store habituates by event kind, and boredom is felt below the line. Actions are scored by HF's `scoreAll` and sampled by softmax from the RNG in the Person's state. All actions share one base promise. Tags come only from the action's class, the person's own recent inputs (`novel`), and, in the task, whether `seen` shows the action advancing the task. | none |
| `hf-no-boredom` | the same code with the one boredom `feel` skipped | none |
| `llm-claude` | Claude Sonnet via `claude -p`, all tools, MCP and settings disabled, run in an empty directory. It plans one to eight actions per call and replans on news. | 40 calls, 10 per episode, one of each kept for notes |
| `llm-gemma` | `@cf/google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it` on Workers AI, thinking off, same prompt and protocol | 40 calls, 10 per episode |
| `llm-gemma-open` | the same model, allowed to call almost every step | 260 calls, $0.15 |
| `scripted` | a greedy carrier of the nearest visible cube, for the task episode only; rests in free time; a baseline, not a proven ceiling | none |

**Determinism (confirmed).** Each `random`, `hf` and `hf-no-boredom` run was made twice per seed. The two recordings were byte-identical (SHA-256 compared). An LLM recording replays exactly because the world is deterministic and the recording holds every action, but rerunning an LLM arm gives a new recording.

**Measures (free time unless noted).**

- cells visited
- distinct action ids
- action-kind entropy
- repeat-with-variation: the share of action-kind 4-grams that recur with different arguments
- idle share
- the most objects on the Spire
- the tallest stack
- ring glows and rock cubes
- the task score
- HF's mean boredom per episode

One caution: repeat-with-variation does not separate play from noise. Random scored 0.56 to 0.68 on it, second only to Gemma with an open budget (0.68 to 0.74), which spent its time picking up and putting down cubes.

## What to change before a second run

- **Fix the HF adapter first.**
  - Record object positions as place-linked memories.
  - Credit outcomes to the action that earned them.
  - Judge novelty by effect, not by input id.
  - Raise routine stimulation, or lengthen the engagement half-life, so boredom can rise and fall.
  - Run an arm without the task interpreter.
- **Run a matched LLM comparison.** Add one uncapped Claude arm, or compare arms at an equal number of chosen actions.
- **Make the forced rests explicit.** Show them more plainly in the LLM history (for example "you were made to rest: no calls left this hour") and check whether the self-blame persists.
- **Test discovery directly.** Give each mind the diagnostic actions (a hard rock throw, three placements in the ring) and see whether it learns from them. Do not just run longer.
- **Run more seeds and a second Claude seed** before claiming any difference between arms.

## Review

A Codex contrarian review (2026-10-20) checked the first draft against the code and recordings. I accepted every point after checking the numbers against the recordings and the code:

- the wandering and manipulation shares;
- Gemma's jumps and throws, all in the task episode;
- boredom by episode;
- that the forced-rest reason was in the prompt;
- that `proposeGoals` and place-linked memory exist in HF 2.9.0;
- that the HF task scores include the hand-written interpreter.

The first draft named "self-set projects" and "remembered places" as missing HF faculties. The reviewer's verdict was that this "does not survive as stated". This version narrows them to hypotheses, to be tested with a better adapter.
