**2. Is it fun? Construction has enough interest for a short break. The new safety-checking activity does not sustain it. I would not choose a whole run for this loop.**

The interesting decisions are where to excavate, where light is needed, and whether protection should depend on distance or a door someone might close. A shaft trades air for another entrance. Those decisions change a visible place. I enjoyed Elif opening the wall I marked and later extending a protected room. This preserves the plan → named worker → physical change that all three players valued.

The change adds information and a harmless first encounter, rather than a new action. During a raid, inspecting eight adults who are eating, sleeping or resting does not give me eight interesting decisions. My practical options remain wait, repeat the bell, or leave the inspection and dig farther. The new room reading makes the last option much better. The door list mostly makes the wait more explicit.

The safety display also emphasizes a quantity that is not the sheltering constraint. Sleeping has three-person cell capacity, but `deepestSafe` selects a reachable open cell without shelter occupancy or capacity. Marked safe beds therefore do not determine how many people can take the shelter action. Conversely, ordinary “sleep below” selects the nearest available bed and advertises safety during danger even if that bed is exposed. In my second warning I had nine protected sleeping places, yet many people were sleeping in shallow exposed beds. Removing those marks was a useful correction, but the game's own offered sleeping option was not using the risk distinction it had just taught me. See sleep selection (driver scratch) and shelter destination (driver scratch).

At 12:20:11 my protected room held twenty-four, but the village had grown to forty-one; at 12:22:29 it had forty-nine. Expanding beds was becoming an obligation to match a growing number, rather than a different spatial problem. For a whole run, the new mechanic supplies no fresh decision after the topology is understood. A full-year plain-bot run reached the year goal with 37 injury events and no deaths. That cannot measure enjoyment. It does show why “the village survived” is a weak acceptance test for this design. The year goal is elapsed time, not an evaluated refuge outcome; this change did not alter it. None of the three supplied testers reached an ending, so there is no evidence that a beloved ending was lost. See year goal (driver scratch).

**3. New quit points and strategies.**

- **The first-raid success is unearned.** Scouting suppresses all harm and theft, then the unchanged `held` calculation awards successful-defence reputation and `heldAt` anyway. This raises reputation and doubles the arrival multiplier for fifteen days when space permits. Doing nothing about the scouts earns the same defence reward as protecting everyone. Ignoring the first encounter is therefore a viable low-effort policy; I have not proved it globally dominant because bell trust, later behaviour and building goals can differ. In my run all nine founders were recorded as people who “would have been caught,” then the journal checked off surviving the first riders. Families subsequently arrived saying they had heard “the hill held.” See scout consequences (driver scratch), held reward (driver scratch), and arrivals (driver scratch).
- **The door help can invite a false alarm.** At 12:14:20 it said to ring so someone could shut the door. I followed it before smoke. The stone stayed open; the journal then said “No riders came … [they] looked at the bell, and then at you.” Quiet-time sheltering can incur autonomy/leisure penalties and affect trust. The new actionable-sounding help points into that existing penalty without explaining the cost. That is a worse learning experience than an honest unavailable action. See door-help.ts:27 (driver scratch) and false-alarm shelter outcome (driver scratch).
- **The journal becomes repetitive work.** After ten people were hurt in my second raid, opening the journal at 12:19:14 showed repeated “The wound on my … is easier today. It still aches” entries. The menu only retains the latest sixty qualifying notes in its view. Recovery reporting is humane, but this volume displaces the construction and raid history I wanted to interpret. At that point I would close the game on a break. See recovery narration (driver scratch) and journal window (driver scratch).

**4. What did it cost?** People's autonomy and HF decision rules remain intact; the patch does not turn the door into an order. Their existing speech survives. However, numerous identical scouting and healing sentences compete with the particular words and construction stories players liked. The first raid loses all material stakes, including the satisfying distinction Bao noticed between stolen near jars and surviving far jars. That is a defensible teaching cost only if scouting leads to a successful repair before the next raid. It currently also grants growth rewards irrespective of exposure. Later danger is unchanged, so the patch can move the same frustration to a larger village rather than resolve it.

**5. Ranked changes for one round each.**

1. **Keep a genuine raid call active until the all-clear.** Test one bell starting the suggestion for that raid; retain individual refusals and independent choices throughout subsequent HF reviews. Quiet-time calls can still expire as false alarms. Remove the routine renewal demand while the same raid continues, and make quiet-time door help state the false-alarm cost. Reason: this directly addresses Mirela's actual quit statement and lets me observe a response instead of servicing a timer. Acceptance: one smoke-time ring, an entire raid, and no “Ring to renew” nag or renewal interrupts; people can still refuse throughout the raid. This is a proposal to test, not a demonstrated retention fix.
2. **Do not count a harmless scouting visit as successful defence.** Keep its search tracks and named exposure report, but skip the `heldAt`/defence-reputation award for that encounter. Reason: immunity must not teach that the exposed refuge worked or accelerate reward-driven growth. Acceptance: a watch-only scouting visit leaves the defence bonus unset; actual later successful defence still earns it.
3. **During danger, prefer a reachable available bed beyond current rider search, then the nearest such bed.** Change bed selection, not the person's decision to accept sleep, shelter or another activity. Preserve capacity and route constraints, and avoid preferring stale-air space; current rider protection alone does not establish a safe journey. Reason: building a demonstrably protected room should make the offered “sleep below” option reflect that protection. Acceptance: a layout with nearby exposed beds and farther reachable protected beds offers the latter during danger; refusal and personal priorities remain possible. This gives the player's geometry an observable consequence without ordering anyone.
