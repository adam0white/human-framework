## Clarity findings

**DL-19: “The same brief demand display change happened again … it still makes the click look worse than it is.”** Immediate visible-text readings after price clicks sometimes showed a demand percentage roughly a tenth of the later settled display. At 1:29, raise to $0.27 showed “Public Demand: 3.08%”; at 1:33 the display was 29%. At 12:31, lower from $0.16 to $0.15 showed “Public Demand: 5.50%”; at 13:00 it was 58%. A lower price initially appeared to collapse demand. Diary entries at 1:33, 1:39 and 12:34 preserve the reactions and the click times. This is an observed transient cue problem, not evidence that lower prices actually reduce settled demand. **The transient was captured in the visible-text transcript, not in a saved screenshot; its duration was not measured.** The later stable state is visible in [marketing effect](n2-clips-v2-shots/08-marketing-effect.png) and [stop](n2-clips-v2-shots/09-stop.png).
