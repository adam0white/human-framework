## What it felt like

**Best moment:** Joss’s decision. The game finally asked something I cared about: whether an exhausted person should stay. It slowed without making me hunt for pause. I could read the warning, think about Yunus covering the same wall, and choose with time left. The 107-second prompt fell to 76 seconds over 30.866 real seconds before my click. It stayed open. Days returned afterwards, with a short intervening slowdown (30–34).

**Another strong moment:** Kian’s “Nothing wrong with me” after leaving the wall. I took it as a person deflecting, not a reliable medical assessment. That is my interpretation of the dialogue.

**Worst moment:** after Let Joss go. The choice vanished but I could not connect it to a clear departure or acknowledgment. He still appeared asleep at the mill, and the off-wall list named only Kian. Dawn said he had helped defend that wall. I cannot tell whether my input permitted a later choice, failed, or had an effect I missed. I am not claiming a broken simulation (32–35).

**Boredom:** first stated at 3:27, when I chose Days. The first two nights involved long stretches of watching people handle wolves themselves. The character conversations kept me interested more than moving the light did.

**Frustration:** mild, first with the phone preparation controls and later with the uncertain Joss result. I did not rage-quit or reach the eight-minute ceiling.
