## Best, worst, and loss of patience

**Best:** My first chalk mark became a real extension, with Elif named in the consequence. Marking it as storage then produced jars and a completed harvest objective. That was a clear sequence from my plan to people's work. The shelter check was also directly relevant to what I cared about: it explained beds, depth, doors, entrances, and current exposure.

**Worst:** Trying to extend the sleeping room. The plan was already small, and the map shifted when the top warning wrapped and rescaled as the underground shape changed. I hit open passage, disconnected stone, and apparently an existing bed square while trying to make room for six more people. Screenshots 14–23 show that sequence.

**First friction:** About 47.7 seconds, when I had not noticed the tiny accepted chalk mark. This resolved when Elif dug it.

**Frustration:** By 147.3 seconds, the changing map position was costing attention. At 197.2 seconds I explicitly preferred shelter planning to square-finding. At 269.1 seconds the failed attempt to shut the door became the reason to leave. I was not bored by an uneventful timer; I was losing patience with precision and an unresolved control.

1. Let me place a room accurately on the phone. Give me a closer view or another clear way to select small cells, and keep the location I am working on stable while the prompt changes.

3. Keep the shelter capacity and exposure check easy to consult while I build. The current check is useful; I want to see which sleeping room is safe before I call people into it.
