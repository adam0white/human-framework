## Uncertainties and coding boundaries

- Read D01–D41 in order, one complete diary at a time. Saved each row before opening the next. D03 and D07 outputs were truncated; recovered only their missing bounded time segments before coding. No other diary output was truncated.
- Times are the raw record `elapsed_s` at the reaction, not inferred game dates, input-start times, or rounded report times. These establish when the words were recorded, not necessarily when the feeling first began. Minor raw/report timing discrepancies are not additional events.
- D32 first names an already-existing wish while saying it eased. Its onset has no measured time. D21 has an earlier inquiry about leaving safely before an unequivocal wish. Both limits remain in their rows.
- D07, D22, D38 and D40 link stopping to a second reread. Their wording supports a rule-associated stop, not a clean estimate of spontaneous desire independently of protocol. Required layout checks, Feedback inspection, and staying for time set aside are obligations or procedures, not evidence that a wish vanished.
- D03, D26, D29 and D41 explicitly describe passage or temporary interruption and later recurrence. Other diaries sometimes regain interest, care, satisfaction, or a reason to stay without establishing that their wish fully passed. “Eased a little” is partial easing.
- D23 contains earlier retrospective stop-event labels without actual leaving words. Those labels were ignored. D38 likewise has a label attached to reread/confusion before its first actual stopping words. Quoted Feedback button names alone are not wishes.
- No lack of an earlier wish was coded as enjoyment. Positive reactions were used only where actually expressed. A final decision to stop, temporary unattended play, pausing, closing, and possible later return remain distinct.
- Retrospective section 3 was read as part of each diary. Repetitions of raw reactions were not counted as new wishes, and retrospective-only wording was not assigned an invented measured onset. No cross-diary identity, mapping, source behavior, or player-test inference was used.
