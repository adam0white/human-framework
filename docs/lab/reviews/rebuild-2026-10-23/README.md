## Limits of the evidence

The first-minute reaction notes in all 19 sessions (18 lab sessions and the control) were logged late, from 66.4 to 81.1 seconds after driver open, typically after a pending wait. This does not meet the mandatory 60-second reaction-note timing. Reports preserve exact times and restrict checkpoint judgments to evidence reached before 60 seconds. Automatic screenshots exist at 60 seconds, but an uninspected screenshot does not establish a visual result. These timing deviations are not repaired by later understanding.

The Small Works access brief initially omitted the driver's optional tap `role`, needed to address tabs by name because the default addresses buttons and links. Ellis tried `meter` twice and stopped while still wanting to play. Later briefs documented roles. Treat that stop as an instrument limitation. Neighbours also ended on an expandable-control access failure. Voice could not open Game menu after stopping, so feedback was blocked. Both Watch feedback name expanders were reported visible but failed exact-text taps twice; neither Watch session submitted feedback. Some other testers opened the disclosure by a screenshot-based coordinate tap. These facts do not establish that a human cannot operate the controls.

Driver reading and input occur intermittently. Game clocks, real elapsed time and response latency are separate. A disabled control or a changed label is game state, not a driver block. Reports record incomplete asks, untested interactions, and cases where timing, layout, or delivery could not be established. No replay validity is claimed.

The coordinator captured release metadata separately from play, with curl after Python requests received HTTP 403. The initial Rope and Small Works captures occurred after their sessions began. Subsequent capture timestamps and full responses remain in driver scratch. All 18 lab start/end commit captures matched and reported clean builds. This does not pin the network deployment continuously. A Dark Room’s `/release.json` returned HTTP 404; its build is unavailable.

Reports retain testers' own words. Local paths were replaced with “(driver scratch)”; screenshots, filled prompts, raw process output and full diaries remain there. Proposed ledger rows are handed to the consolidator separately; this round does not edit `feedback-ledger.md`.

## Cross-game patterns

**Named consequences sustained interest, sometimes after a wish to leave.** Mum carrying the repaired lantern was Ellis’s best moment in [Small Works](repair-free.md). In [The Pass continued session](pass-continued.md), Dilek’s emergency temporarily eased Tomas’s wish to leave. [Watch](watch-continued.md) offered a similar return of interest when calling a sleeping watcher felt like a rescue. [Valley](valley-free.md) made a refusal meaningful through Zehra’s “Our own barley first.” These are specific moments to preserve when evaluating changes; they do not show that more exposure always restores interest.

**Wanting an action and finding its control were separate.** Tomas wanted a conversation beyond a job request in [Pass](pass-free.md). Omar sought a family-related act in [Voice](voice-free.md). Cem wanted sharing choices in [Stairwell](stairwell-free.md), but reported a blank page. Leena could issue orders in [Well](well-free.md), yet could not tell which intervention produced food. Those experiences have different causes to reproduce; the common evidence is the gap between an intended act and a legible way to do or understand it.

**Phone context still went missing in sampled views.** Reports identify a truncated help hint in [Rope](rope-free.md), agreement text on [Pass’s board](pass-free.md), a missing deadline on [Good Hands’ proposal sheet](goodhands-free.md), and truncated goal/explanation text in [Valley](valley-free.md). Each report distinguishes screenshots from accessible text. Unseen views and unmeasured layouts remain untested.

**Access failures constrain the comparison.** Small Works, Neighbours and both Valley sessions stopped under the tool rule while the player still wanted to continue. Well’s continued arm also ended at a disclosure control before its floor. Watch and Voice had separate feedback-access failures after voluntary gameplay stops. These stops and missing feedback links are instrument limitations, not evidence that those games lost their players. In particular, the continued-exposure comparison is incomplete for Well and Valley.

**Time spent and the wish to leave differ.** The blind reader found early wishes followed by continued play, temporary recoveries, and qualified willingness to return. The Two Voices also had an assigned away interval; the reader’s early-break code must be read with that assignment caveat, alongside the later unambiguous wish to end. Glance has an ambiguous earlier “losing me” line before its definite stop. No mean stopping time or ranking is inferred from this small, unbalanced round.

**The control also produced an idle-and-return wish.** In [A Dark Room](adr-free.md), Vera’s first wish at 5:04.5 eased when the cart and hut appeared. She stopped active tending at 7:23.3 and would leave it running and return after tea. She reported understandable wood costs and enjoyable sparse discoveries, alongside cooldown uncertainty and intermittent-driver limits. This is one desktop control, not a repeated calibration trend or a matched phone comparison. It cautions against treating every BORED stop as abandonment or as proof of a lab-game defect.

The reports identify moments to reproduce and wishes to consider. Owner play and acceptance remain distinct from this evidence. No game changes were made in this round.
