Preserve delivered Service Day 0.8 as a frozen control, including final core `0a48e9f`, its replay/save contract, the 18 comparison trials and two carryover branches. The verified 0.8 deployment source is `afe95c7009f88b82fc6391d269014d2cc500d79e`. [Exact release evidence](release-0.8.md) binds the public manifest. **Recommendation:** add one separate Service Day continuation with a single active clinic agreement and one pending revision, implemented first as a direct host record. Do not add a planner, trust score, generic commitment API, radio network or memory package in this milestone.

## The concrete demand, including a stronger existing control

The recorded gate carryover reaches minute 41 with a keeper-owned spare and one pump section done. Its frozen controller rests the keeper until 47; Deniz starts the cart at 42 and occupies the clinic's only receiving slot. That is a controller coordination failure, not physical impossibility or evidence that Deniz cannot reason like a person. [Existing evidence](service-day-comparison.md) describes the exact limitation.

A new independent exploratory control already fixes that outcome through current legal requests. From the same minute-41 checkpoint, request both people rest; stop/restart Deniz's requested rest at 46, start keeper pump at 47, stop/restart Deniz's rest at 51, then stop that rest and request Deniz deliver at 53. **Two units arrive at 59, morning water remains served, Deniz pays 12 rest minutes.** Every command is accepted and every intervening save resumes exactly. Preserve [executable probe](../artifacts/service-day/late-timed-request-control.mjs) and [full provenance/checkpoint/commands/result](../artifacts/service-day/late-timed-request-control.json). This is post-comparison exploratory evidence, not another reserved result or an optimal schedule.

The proposed benefit is saying “I will finish the pump; will you wait and deliver?” with an explicit answer and revision, instead of repeatedly using recovery requests to keep the partner available. It has not been established that a person finds the explicit form clearer or more enjoyable.

## Smallest playable interaction

Use a separate host/version and route; leave `/service/` and its saves untouched. Keep the same two obligations, ownership, task durations, body/practice costs and one clinic slot.

Choose **co-located discussion** first. The current host publicly exposes jobs, work and inventory; inventing ignorance just to justify a radio would bias the experiment. “Discuss clinic plan” occupies both consenting idle people for **two paid active minutes each**, with no recovery credit. Deniz may refuse the invitation because own work is underway or the discussion would consume the remaining feasible cart window. Invitation consent is permission to listen, not acceptance of the proposed terms. Only completion supplies a response; interruption preserves paid time and leaves the previous agreement intact. Starting a discussion never pauses an unwilling person's job or the world's clock.

One immutable proposal states: keeper will achieve a named readiness condition by `readyBy`; Deniz will wait until `waitUntil`, then deliver if ready or apply the specified fallback. Show the actual part owner, installed work and whether a part is merely promised. Acceptance binds the exact revision. It transfers no inventory, reserves no physical part, guarantees no capacity, and does not discharge the clinic obligation. The keeper remains free to change work and incur recorded nonperformance. Deniz retains independent recovery and refusal.

Offer two visibly different terms:

- **Protect one-unit fallback:** hold through minute 45; if pump/supply are ready choose full delivery, otherwise start the cart. Cart 45–63 is feasible; cart starting 46 loses to closing. A proposed 37–39 discussion followed by keeper pump 39–45 gives a concrete full-water route, subject to legal prefix and actual capacity checks before preregistration.
- **Risk the fallback for full service:** explicitly wait past 45 for a later pump completion. For the retained gate branch, propose discussion 41–43, paid keeper rest 43–47, pump 47–53 and Deniz delivery 53–59. These new-host timings are design arithmetic, not executed results. Confirm actual capacity and recipient acceptance before freezing them. If the promise fails, zero units is a legitimate outcome; do not secretly grant a late cart.

Deniz's first controller uses transparent deadline/resource arithmetic: accept a feasible one-unit-preserving hold; consider a risk-bearing extension only with ready inlet, an owned or installed required part, an explicit keeper schedule whose forecast full delivery is before 64, and no conflicting own work. Decline missing ownership, incompatible terms or a latest full start later than 57. Forecasts are advisory; recipient consent is not omniscient proof that execution will succeed. Expose the authored acceptance rule, including its willingness to risk the fallback, instead of labeling it universal rationality.

The player can propose a revision when both can discuss. Until a revision is accepted, the existing terms remain active. Declined, interrupted, expired or stale responses cannot cancel those terms. Keep one pending revision and one current accepted plan, with monotonic identities, proposer/recipient, creation/response times, superseded identity, promised time and actual host completion receipt. Repeated latest-only refusal summaries must coalesce with their replay commands. Track missed terms as facts, without moral scoring or inferring motive.

Initially allow renegotiation **before cart departure only**. The keeper is not the clinic beneficiary and cannot release its obligation; a bilateral discussion cannot reclaim an already committed receiving slot. “Please stop the cart” may receive a visible refusal after departure. Physical return, slot release and third-party clinic consent would be a later design, not an invisible free cancellation.

## Age and event ordering without unnecessary memory machinery

Label “proposed at 37,” “accepted at 39,” “promised ready by 45” and current physical readiness separately. A promise is not an observation or a fulfillment receipt. Existing directly visible work should stay directly visible. Last Light's useful lesson is source-time versus receipt-time and stale-message protection, not evidence that its memory candidate should be imported. Test duplicate/old-revision receipts, but do not describe this synchronous meeting as delayed-report cognition. Add asynchronous communication only after a separate-site task actually needs it.

Write priority explicitly: install the elapsed work minute; process the existing morning/closing events first; settle completed work and discussion receipts; then evaluate accepted-plan deadlines and each actor's next choice. At 45, ready pump progress can support full delivery; otherwise the agreed cart starts. A discussion finishing after a plan's expiry cannot revive it. Nothing finishing at closing can defeat the existing closing-first rule. Save mid-discussion, mid-revision and on those ties must preserve exact order.

## Discriminating experiment and rejection gates

Compare three distinct questions, without pooling their outcomes:

1. **Gameplay/control:** frozen 0.8 fixed fallback, the verified competent timed-request control, and the new direct-record agreement controller. Same initial legal prefixes, physical costs and visible facts. Different communication/recovery schedules are measured interventions, not claimed matched exposure. Include an additional “notice the other actor's already-running pump” host rule: if that trivial rule resolves a case, preserve the result against a broader planning claim.
2. **Representation, only if extraction is proposed:** direct host record versus an adapter around the private social ledger, with identical paid discussions, information, acceptance rules, revisions and physical receipts. Require exact world/decision/save continuation parity. The previous social probe already found equal outcomes with larger shared state; do not repeat it merely to get two imports. Measure inclusive adapter/helper/validation source and saved state. If the direct record suffices, ship it and reject extraction.
3. **Human legibility:** later accept actual supplied notes asking what was agreed, what changed, why Deniz refused and which fallback was lost. No supplied human feedback on this feature exists. Developer/agent trials cannot close the five-person explanation gate.

Freeze six development cases and four authored reserved cases before implementation outcomes. Development: no agreement/current fallback; feasible short hold; missed promise with cart at 45; feasible explicitly risky hold; accepted revision; refused revision leaving old plan. Reserved: interrupted discussion retains paid costs and old terms; capacity-refused execution after an accepted advisory forecast; an old response after a newer revision; a revision/deadline tie or post-departure request. Use valid paid prefixes, never body resets or manufactured parts. Print every case, including zero-unit failures, rather than a weighted winner.

Record both service outcomes/time, owned/reserved/installed resources, each person's paid work/rest/discussion, agreement/revision/refusal times and reasons, promised versus actual readiness, cart-slot commitment, lost/retained work, active save bytes, command count and exact replay equality. Compare source size inclusively; do not call it measured authoring effort. Retain the existing 64 KiB save bound and reserve sufficient journal capacity for interruption and closing.

**Reject or narrow the milestone** if timed requests or the trivial visible-pump rule achieve the same useful behavior with clearer controls; if every accepted plan automatically succeeds; if all improvements come from free communication, hidden peer knowledge or changed deadlines; if ownership/consent/slot conservation fails; if repeated revisions break bounded continuation; or if no case benefits from revision rather than one initial request. Reject a general planning/commitment faculty unless a distinct consumer later demands it and a fair direct-record comparison demonstrates benefit. The present proposal can earn a small playable coordination mechanism while rejecting architectural expansion.

The independent critique emphasized these same risks: paid consent is not free interruption, promises are not truth, the keeper cannot discharge the clinic's claim, minute 45 includes all communication costs, and a direct record deserves the full set of affordances. Root selects this as the next milestone under the user’s existing autonomous authorization. This is a design proposal, not implementation or a frozen outcome protocol; refine it into concrete predeclared cases before evaluating new outcomes.
