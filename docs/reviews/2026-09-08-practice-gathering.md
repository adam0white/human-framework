## The consequential comparison

The common prefix completes one timber trip and one forage trip. At minute 30 it holds seven timber, two salvage and six food, with player gathering skill 0.22999673, fatigue .375 and hunger .28. Meryem remains unrequested and pays automatic recovery in every arm. The objective is the **timber component** for both woodshed stages. Every arm holds ten timber and two salvage at 60. All have player hunger .34, total declared effort .26, construction exposure zero and construction skill .05. The three continuously productive/practice arms have identical gathering skill 0.34121662; the recovery control has 0.32033858. Tiny floating-point body/effort differences among the first three arms are retained in the raw snapshots and are not practical body advantages. Meryem's final person state is exactly equal across arms: fatigue zero, hunger .40, no job, unchanged skills and sixty paid recovery minutes.

The source maps paid exposure `p` to `1 - .9 * exp(-.0052*p)` for this initial gathering skill, then admits a fixed timber duration of `16 - floor(skill*4)`. Six forage minutes raise skill from 0.22999673 to 0.25364992 before the later timber job starts, crossing the .25 threshold and shortening that new trip from sixteen to fifteen minutes. Completing the whole forage also crosses it and earns food. Recovering for six minutes does not practice gathering. The quoted-duration consequence therefore follows directly from the source and recorded admission values; it is not inferred from the final score or attributed to a changed workbench, actor or resource bonus.

The partial contender arrives one minute earlier than the equally delayed recovery arm, but its six activity minutes replace real recovery. At 60 its fatigue is .15 higher. Forage's zero declared effort does **not** mean zero fatigue maintenance or recovery: active minutes still pay fatigue and hunger maintenance. Food is earned only at completed forage, and no arm eats, so extra stocked portions have not been converted into hunger relief.

Against the stronger productive controls, canceling is a tradeoff rather than a demonstrated exploit. It gets timber eight minutes before finishing forage first, but forfeits that forage's two-food completion. Starting timber immediately is faster still: it obtains the objective at 46, then earns another two food while reaching the same cumulative practice/body at 60. Cancellation's shorter *later trip* must not be presented as faster fulfillment than this direct useful-work control.

The pending work matters. At 60 the partial contender has nine paid minutes in its active thirteen-minute forage, compared with one minute in the two productive controls. That output remains unearned, yet the more advanced active trip may affect a later choice. The current record does not execute or optimize those future continuations. It supports “earlier stated target and more current food in the productive timber-first control,” not “strictly better in every physical dimension forever.” Minute 60 is the fixed observation time, not a game deadline.

## Admission scope

Keep the exact practice consequence and the negative incentive finding. There is no evidence here for a new learning mechanism, proficiency meter or change to interruption credit. A concise explanation that paid useful work can shorten later jobs, and that food gathering is light work, could make the existing choice easier to understand; UI admission belongs to the separate UI inspection and must avoid implying that forage restores the body. This one selected early opportunity and its four controls do not establish that every possible practice strategy is well behaved. No further gathering nomination or expanded search was used to reach this conclusion.
