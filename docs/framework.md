# HF (Human Framework) — architecture

This is the design reference for `packages/human` (`@adam0white/human-framework`). For installation, a quick start and the host contract in brief, see the [package README](../packages/human/README.md). The [API reference](api.md) lists every export. The shared data contract lives in [`packages/human/src/types.ts`](../packages/human/src/types.ts). Modules expose deterministic readers and mutators over a `Person`. Faculty modules mutate only their owned slices; composites coordinate calls across faculties.

## The spine: Urge → Assent → Act

A person does not execute commands. At each decision point, many pulls act on them at once:

- bodily needs (hunger, thirst, sleep, pain)
- psychological needs (belonging, autonomy, competence, meaning, ...)
- understood duties and commitments
- habits
- emotions and their action tendencies
- goals
- learned expectations of what each action will actually do
- suggestions from outside voices (a player, a parent, a manager)

These pulls are *urges*. The person weighs them and *assents* to one course of action, or refuses a suggested one, and then *acts*. The host world resolves what actually happens. The outcome feeds back into body, memory, skills, emotions, relationships, conscience, and trust in the voices that advised them.

This structure follows a distinction in the project's [Islamic foundations](../research/islamic-foundations.md) (§3–4). An internal suggestion is not yet a deed (Bukhari 5269). Responsibility attaches to deliberate assent and action, and it is bounded by capacity (Qur'an 2:286). Intention is recorded separately from the outward act (Bukhari 1).

In the software, a player's input is therefore one urge among many: a push, not a command. This is the framework's signature mechanic, and it is useful outside games too. The same machinery models a manager directing workers, a parent instructing a child, or public-health advice reaching a population.

Boundaries the software never crosses: it does not compute divine acceptance, assign quantities to the ruh, or call the RNG "qadar". Norms come from a host catalog with provenance. The person holds an *understanding* of each norm, and that understanding is not a ruling.

## Why a suggestion lost (opt-in)

`enableSuggestionReasons(p)` or `PersonSpec.enable.suggestionReasons` enables
`SuggestionResolution.decidedBy` on score-based deferrals and modifications. Its rounded `margin`
is the winner's score minus the closest suggested option's score, both including activity inertia. `inertia`
means removing that bonus would make a suggested option win; otherwise `term` names the largest
positive difference between their terms, summed by source and tied by source string. Vetoes, outcomes imposed by
insistence and stochastic wins below the best live score have no score explanation. Verdicts, `reason`, wording and RNG stay
unchanged; without the opt-in, resolution JSON stays byte-identical.

A self abstention commitment can set `firm: true`. While pending, non-exempt and overlapping the
action, it vetoes the abstained action as `refused/willNot`, reason `rule:<label>` (id fallback),
including self choice and insistence. Self means `toId` absent, `self` or the person's id. A linked
obligatory norm keeps its existing duty veto instead. The existing necessity threshold lifts the
veto only for food or water actions, when necessity is enabled. This is a held personal rule, not a
religious ruling. Without `firm`, the existing commitment terms and verdicts stay unchanged.

## Curiosity and play (opt-in)

`enableCuriosity(p, spec?)` or `PersonSpec.enable.curiosity` adds intrinsic exploration through
`curiosity:unknown`, `curiosity:progress` and `curiosity:vary`, in that order. Unknown uses memory's
expectation confidence; progress is the positive difference between slow and fast prediction-error
averages; variation invites a recent action at a different target or place, unless that exact repeat
was also recent. Physiological urgency, safety and fear suppress the common drive; openness and
maturity scale it. `curiosityOf` reads the raw signals and drive without mutation or RNG.

`curiosity/` owns at most 32 expectation-key histories and eight recent completed or failed acts.
`finish` samples error before memory updates, then rewards leisure and competence through `needs/`,
with a combined cap of 0.03 by default. Interrupted acts do neither. The defaults are engineering
choices, not fitted values; [research/curiosity-and-play.md](../research/curiosity-and-play.md) records
the scientific shapes. This slice reads no `novel` tag and changes no boredom, projects, planning or
faith mechanic. Absent the slice, state and legacy continuation stay identical.

## Persistence (opt-in)

`enablePersistence(p, spec?)` or `PersonSpec.enable.persistence` retains one started active goal in
`p.pursuit`. `begin` uses the begun option's positive `goal:<id>` terms, including planned routes,
to form or refresh it. Largest term wins, tied by goal id. Switching goals requires the new term to
exceed the current goal's term on that option (zero if absent) by more than 0.15. Strength is goal
importance × (0.4 + 0.6 × conscientiousness). `consider` adds `pursuit:<id>` to means serving it and
`pursuit:switch` against alternatives, including rest. Both use scale 0.8, cap 0.8 and the relaxed
physiological/safety/fear gate; options carrying `commitment:` terms never pay switching cost.
`tick` and `finish` release achieved, abandoned, missing, unserved for 20 minutes or 240-minute-old
pursuits. Maximum-duration release retains one refractory entry for the most recent release,
with a 20-minute interval (`restMinutes`); that goal cannot form a pursuit while the entry remains.
With defaults (`maxMinutes` 240, `restMinutes` 20), two releases cannot fall within one interval;
with a shorter maximum, a later release can replace an earlier unexpired interval.
Its agenda terms stay unchanged. Refreshing service keeps the original start time. A short interruption permits resumption.
`pursuitOf` returns a read-only copy. These are engineering defaults: a bounded bias plus a switching
cost, not a planner or a change to goal valuation. See [research/projects-and-persistence.md](../research/projects-and-persistence.md).
Newly enabled persistence (`behaviour: 2`) halves both terms every 30 minutes without the pursued goal's net progress; the goal clock survives release and re-formation, while restored unmarked slices retain the 2.14.0 pull until re-enabled.
Without this slice the existing state, terms and continuation digests stay identical.

## Loop

```
host world ──affordances──▶ decide(person, affordances, {suggestion})
     ▲                         │  score every option: Σ terms (needs, norms, commitments, goals,
     │                         │  habits, emotions, expectations, social, effort, risk, suggestion)
     │                         │  will.resolve: vetoes, self-control, softmax(rng), suggestion verdict
     │                         ▼
     │                    DecisionRecord (+ narration, intention)
     │                         │
     │                    begin(person, affordance, decision) → person.activity
     │                         │
     │   tick(person, now) ◀───┘  closed-form body/needs/affect/memory decay; works for any dt
     │                         │
     └──────── outcome ─────── finish(person, outcome) → body, needs, skills, habits, memory,
               percepts ────── perceive(person, percepts) → attention → beliefs, social, affect
```

Decisions are event-driven: a person decides when idle, when an activity ends, or when the host interrupts them. Between decisions, `tick` advances continuous state in closed form within each segment. It splits elapsed time at activity ends and on the absolute `PERSON_DEFAULTS.maxSegment` grid (60 minutes by default). Its cost is O(elapsed segments), with per-segment work depending on the stored records and enabled faculties.

## Beliefs and trust

HF keeps three separate trust stores. `social.relationships[].trust` is trust in a person as a partner; authored ties (`seedTie`, `seedRelationships`, `meet`), experienced social events (including forgiveness), observed acts (`judge`) and reputation testimony (`perceive` / `applyReputationBelief`) can move it. `will.voices[].trust` is trust in an advising voice; `seedVoice` authors it, outcomes of followed advice, insisting and commands move it, `noteVoicePromise` updates it when a voice keeps or breaks its word, and upbringing can pass on household voice trust through `adoptVoiceTrust`. `memory.sourceTrust`, read by `trustOf`, is trust in an information source; `seedSourceTrust` authors it and `confirm` recalibrates it against observed truth. `believe` and testimony received through `perceive` weight claims by this source trust. These stores do not copy trust between them, even when their ids match. `seedBelief` authors a belief directly without applying source trust; its optional source records a claim for later confirmation, while an omitted source records no observation.

See [Host kit](host-kit.md) for host authoring recipes and save bookkeeping.

## Utility of an option

`consider()` sums the following terms. Every term is kept in the trace so the UI can show why.

- **need:X** = urgency(X) × believed gain on X × chance of success × (1 − scarcity effect). The believed gain blends the advertisement with the learned expectation, weighted by how many samples the person has.
- **expectation**: the learned valence of this action × its confidence × relevance (the urgency of the need it has served, when it was pleasant). Absent until the person has tried the action.
- **memory** (opt-in, `enableGists`): what a lasting gist says about this option, weighted by the gist and by how little fresh experience (the expectation's confidence) has replaced it.
- **norm:N**: from conscience. Positive for fulfilling an understood obligation or recommendation, negative for a breach. Weighted by conviction, tradition/conformity, and honesty-humility.
- **commitment:C**: rises as the window closes.
- **precommit:ID**: a precommitment's bias, while the time of day is inside its daily window and the action matches.
- **goal:G**: importance × amount, with existing deadline urgency. Optional `Goal.temporal` also applies expected action success and completion-time discount; one received route can contribute through the same goal amount reader.
- **prospection:cost:source:id**: one selected route's expected successor effort/material cost; unscaled by maturity, with optional planning present bias.
- **habit**: strength of a matching cue.
- **emotion:E**: tendency × tag match. Fear avoids risky options, anger favours confronting, loneliness favours social options, guilt favours repair and worship, boredom favours novel options.
- **social:P**: affection and belonging pull toward options with liked people, and away from disliked ones.
- **avoid:X**: fear aimed at a place (`avoid:<placeId>`) or at a person (`avoid:<sourceId>`, from a threat percept) lowers options at that place or with that person.
- **partner:P** (`partneringTerms`, only for offers tagged `partnering`): warmth and appeal for `court`, readiness for `propose` and `accept-proposal`, `partner:status-quo` for `decline-proposal`, and `partner:unavailable` (negative) while married to someone else or in mourning.
- **effort**: effort × fatigue × (1 − fitness), plus focus × sleepiness.
- **risk**: chance × severity × (fear + emotionality).
- **companion:P** (HF 2.0 L6, opt-in): on a risky offer whose `with` names P and the person holds an impression of P, scale × chance × severity × P's believed steadiness (−1..1).
- **material**: value of gain, scaled by security, achievement, and power.
- **suggestion:V**: strength × voice trust × appeal match. This is never enough on its own to override a veto.
- **suggestion:remembered:V**: standing advice from V that is still remembered and matches the option, × trust in V. It is skipped for a voice that is speaking now about the same option.
- **autonomy** (reactance): on the suggested option only, −scale × pressure above 0.3 × selfDirection × (0.5 + autonomy urgency). People resist being micromanaged; insisting adds to the pressure read.
- **commitment** (promise inertia): on the running activity when it was begun with `begin(..., { promise })` and the promise is pending. Full strength until desperation reaches the necessity threshold, gone 0.15 above it: a promise survives hunger, not starvation.
- **joint:P**: on an offer tagged `joint`, trust in each partner (relationship trust − 0.5).

Emotion terms are named `emotion:<emotion>:<tag>` (e.g. `emotion:fear:risky`): the dominant emotion behind the tendency, then the tag it acts through. Each `Considered` carries the offer's `label`.

`will.resolveChoice` then decides:

1. **Vetoes.** Capacity (exhausted, asleep, dead) and strongly held forbidden norms veto an option. The forbidden-norm veto lifts under genuine necessity: when the need urgency is extreme, the person's understanding of necessity applies (Qur'an 2:173 is the canonical source). Hosts can turn this off. Two further rules:
   - **Omission rule.** A person who holds an *obligatory* norm with conviction ≥ 0.7, and has a pending commitment linked to it that is in the last 25 % of its window, will not take an option that runs past the window's end without fulfilling it. This covers salah (obligatory in the common Sunni understanding the catalog records; a person's own understanding may differ) and any host duty, such as care for dependents or another faith's prayer norm the host defines. Obligation is bounded by capacity (Qur'an 2:286; [Islamic foundations](../research/islamic-foundations.md) §4): the rule applies only while some offered option can still fulfil the commitment, and lifts when desperation reaches the necessity threshold. A plain suggestion that would miss the duty is **deferred** with a counter-offer naming it ("after I pray Maghrib", from `Commitment.label`); an insisted one is **refused/willNot** with reason `norm:<id>`. Insisting cannot override it. Two extensions in 1.9.0 (Game 2 eleventh pass): (a) past the window's end the duty stays protected while an activity that keeps it, begun inside the window, is under way (`agenda.isUnderWay`, the same test that keeps the window open), because a prayer begun in its time counts when finished after it ([decisions](../research/decisions.md), "A prayer begun in its time"); leaving it is then an omission, and only continuing it keeps the duty (a fresh start after the end does not). (b) An activity that would cover a protected duty's whole closing stretch and run past the window's end is reviewed when the stretch begins (`will.dutyReviewAt`, applied by the composite in `begin` and `reviewed` for every mode), so a long option chosen just before the stretch (a sleep 29 minutes before sunrise) is weighed under the rule. Option (b) was chosen over blocking such options up front, which would have vetoed every long option, including a night's sleep under an open Isha, from the moment the window opened. At the review capacity and necessity apply as usual: a sleeper who cannot yet wake for the duty sleeps on.
   - **Episode distrust.** When trust in a voice is below 0.45 *and* the person remembers, within 24 h, a salient bad outcome (valence ≤ −0.2, salience ≥ 0.4) of following that voice at the same action, every suggested option with that action is refused **willNot** `distrust`, with `episodeId` naming the memory. They will not do it on that voice's word, even if they might have chosen it unprompted. The older rule (trust < 0.25 and pressure > 0.6 when the suggestion loses) still applies.
2. **Selection.** Argmax with hysteresis by default: the current activity keeps a decaying inertia bonus, and challengers must beat it by `will.switchMargin`. `will.temperature > 0` opts a person into softmax sampled from their own RNG.
3. **Suggestion verdict.** The verdict is typed so that a refusal never reads as a bug:
   - **assented**: the suggested option won.
   - **deferred** (amber, `notNow`): a more urgent need won. Always carries a counter-offer ("after I eat").
   - **modified** (amber): a near alternative serving the same aim won.
   - **refused**: either `cannot` (grey: capacity, skill, asleep) or `willNot` (red: norm veto, broken trust).
   - **complied**: the voice insisted on a `notNow`. The person does it under protest, at a visible price (autonomy drop, voice pressure, a memory, `activity.protest` for hosts to reduce quality). Insisting never overrides `cannot` or `willNot`.

   Every verdict carries a dominant reason and a first-person line. `predictResponse` gives the same verdict without consuming RNG, so the UI can telegraph it on hover.
4. **Autonomy and trust.** Pushing against preference raises voice pressure and drains the autonomy need. Outcomes the person liked raise trust in the voice that suggested them; harm lowers it faster, and faster still when the person had been overruled (`trustLossComplied`: one bad night under protest takes trust 0.75 → ~0.41). `VoiceRelation.history` keeps the last five events that moved trust (`{at, delta, reason, action}`) for UI trust meters. Outcome episodes of suggested activities record `voiceId`.

## Argument order

Since 2.1 the functions that take a time follow one rule.

- A **host event** is something that happened that the host reports. It takes `at` as the last required positional argument, with optional knobs after it: `glimpse(observer, targetId, signs, at, clarity?)`, `glimpseOf(observer, target, at, clarity?)`, `hear(observer, targetId, key, value, at, weight?)`, `conceive(mother, father, seed, at)`, `retell(teller, listener, at, opts?)`. `acquaintWith`, `court`, `marry`, `betroth` and `widow` follow the same rule.
- The 2.0 options-object forms (`{ at, clarity }`, `{ at, seed }`, `{ at, weight }`, `retell(..., { at, ... })`) still work but are deprecated and will be removed in 3.0.
- A **query evaluated at a time** takes `now` instead: `predictAs` (`opts.now`), `previewCommandAs`, `imagine`, `decide` (`opts.now`).
- `canMarry` keeps `opts.at`, because a positional time after the optional `custom` would be ambiguous.

## Host protocols

- **Space and travel** are host responsibilities. The framework has no spatial model; hosts fold travel into `Affordance.duration` and mark nearby percepts with `Percept.near`.
- **Interrupts.** `interrupt(p, now, reason)` brings the running activity's review forward and the reason reaches `DecisionRecord.interrupt`. `stepCommunity` calls it when a perceived percept has salience ≥ 0.8 and targets the person or is flagged `near` (`near: false` never interrupts; `StepOptions.interruptSalience` changes or disables the threshold). Percepts are pulled at each person's own events, so a host that wants an immediate response calls `interruptPerson(c, p, at, reason)` when it queues the percept (it is safe inside `World` callbacks).
- **Promises at begin.** `begin(p, aff, record, { promise: { importance, toId?, normId?, kind?, label? } })` creates a pending commitment for that action and target until the activity's end plus 30 minutes and links it as `Activity.commitmentId`. Completing keeps it; abandoning breaks it (a missed episode, and a breach if `normId` is set). Under `stepCommunity`, a host supplies these options through the optional `World.beginOptions(p, offer, record)`, called for every begin (joint begins included); its result is passed to `begin`.
- **Joint activities.** An offer tagged `joint` whose `with` names community members starts the protocol in `stepCommunity`: the proposer's choice is not begun; each partner is interrupted the same minute and offered a mirror (`mirrorAffordance`, or the host's `World.mirror`), tagged `joint` with `jointId`. The partner's own decision weighs it (social and `joint:` trust terms). If every partner chooses the mirror, all begin the same minute (`acceptJoint`), with `Activity.jointId` shared; if any declines (`declineJoint`), the proposer re-decides that minute without that offer. A skilled joint task's success is the host's roll; `jointSuccessChance(lead, partners, skill, difficulty)` is `successChance` with the partners' skill × capacity as support, and the skill veto allows a 0.15 wider gap on joint offers.
- **Outcome quality.** `Outcome.quality` (0..1) lowers felt valence and therefore learned expectations; it defaults to 0.7 for work done under protest. `Outcome.fulfills`/`advances` key commitments and goals explicitly and default to the activity's affordance.
- **Body thresholds.** `nextBodyThreshold` runs once at `begin` and at each review, never per tick; `Activity.thresholdAt` caches the minute a perceived need will cross its interrupt threshold (absent when none does within the review horizon). Hosts that step every minute read it.
- **Telegraphs.** `preview(p, affordances, suggestion)` in `sim/` (same as `predict`) gives the verdict without changing state.
- **Answering at once.** `answerNow(p, affordances, suggestion)` (person.ts) answers a suggestion between decision points without touching the running activity. A refusal is committed as a real resolution (will `answerSuggestion`): the voice's pressure, refused counter and `pushed`/`worn` trust costs are booked exactly as a decision would book them, and the chronicle tallies the verdict (`noteAnswer`) without counting a decision; it returns `booked: true`. Any other verdict would mean switching activity, so nothing is written and the result equals `predict`; the host then interrupts and lets the next decision resolve it. It draws no RNG, always takes the argmax outcome, and writes no trace entry. A refusal booked this way while the person carries on is counted, where a quiet review that keeps the activity counts nothing; a host that answers at once should drop a refused suggestion, or a later decision on it counts again.
- **Insiders, outsiders and threats (1.6.0, `social/groups.ts`).** The host names the groups and decides who is in which; factions stay with the host. `joinGroups(p, ['village'], { outsiderStance? })` sets the groups a person counts themself in and their stance toward outsiders (−1 wary .. +1 welcoming; default `(openness − 0.5) + (agreeableness − 0.5)`). `meet(p, otherId, groups)` creates a new tie with insider defaults (affection 0.15, trust 0.6, familiarity 0.1) or outsider ones (affection 0, trust 0.4, each shifted by 0.15 × stance) and records the other's groups; an existing tie keeps its values. `careFor(p, id)` is closeness, raised to 0.3 for an insider: it weights harm to others in `judge` and in the appraisal of what happens to them. A percept may carry `threat: { severity, chance?, sourceId?, aboutId? }`: it becomes a fear prospect of `severity × careFor(aboutId)` (self = 1), × up to 1.5 when a wary person sees an outsider source, aimed at the source (`avoid:<sourceId>`). That fear raises the existing risk term, lowers the `risky` tendency, and options with the source person or at the source place (`avoid:<placeId>`, new in cognition) are avoided. Without `joinGroups` no tie or appraisal changes; a threat percept is new input, so existing runs replay byte for byte.
- **Injury depth (1.6.0, `body/injury.ts`).** Three capacities (moving, manipulation, sight), read with `readCapacities(p)` from the injuries: each impairs by `affects[c] × severity`, multiplied across injuries. `affects` is the injury's own when the host passes one, else `DEFAULT_PARTS[part]` by exact name (leg, legs, foot, feet, arm, arms, hand, hands, shoulder, eye, eyes, head, torso, back), else nothing; the scalar `readBody().capacity` is unchanged. An offer may declare `requires: { moving: 0.5 }`; below it the offer is vetoed `cannot`/`capacity:moving`. Bleeding (`Injury.bleeding`, health per day, host-set) drains health and clots (half-life 18 h, 3 h once tended), integrated exactly per sub-step. `tend(p, id?, quality)` cuts the rate by up to 80% and makes the injury heal 1.5× faster. Downed (`body.downed`): the host's `knockDown(p, { reason?, until? })` (interrupts the activity, remembered) and `standUp(p)`, or derived hourly on the tick grid for a person with `enableDowned(p, { moving?, health? })` below either floor (lifts 0.05 above it). While downed only offers tagged `floor` or `rest` or with mode `sleep` are open (others `cannot`/`downed`, so a voice asking for anything else meets that refusal) and a command ends. Nothing is written to existing callers' injuries, and no one is downed unless the host asks, so existing runs replay byte for byte.
- **Mental breaks (1.6.0, `affect/crisis.ts`).** Opt-in per person: `enableBreaks(p, behaviours)` hands over the break behaviours the host's world can express (`{ id, label?, weight?, actions?, tags?, minutes? }`; an offer tagged `break:<id>` also belongs to that behaviour). The framework owns the state (`affect.crisis`: stress, the break in progress, refractory) and its onset and recovery. Stress comes from a low mood, from controlled hours against one's will and from injuries, and halves over a day. Once an hour on the tick grid, while awake and outside the 12 h refractory, a break starts with hazard `0.15/h × pressure × (0.5 + emotionality)`, where pressure is how far the felt mood is below −0.35 or stress above 0.6. The behaviour is drawn by weight from the person's RNG. During a break only the behaviour's offers are open (others are vetoed `cannot`/`break`; bodily-need offers reopen at survival desperation), every voice is refused `cannot`/`break` with no counters, and a command ends. It ends at its drawn length; sleep counts double and a `comfort` percept from someone close (closeness ≥ 0.3) halves what is left. Onset and recovery are remembered (episode kind `break`). `breakHazard(p)` reads the current hazard for UI. Without `enableBreaks` nothing runs and no randomness is drawn. A prayer missed during a break is recorded as missed, with its breach and a make-up owed: reason is intact in a break, so it stays accountable (1.7.0, research/decisions.md).
- **Impressions (HF 2.0 L6, `social/impressions.ts`, composite `impression.ts`).** Opt-in: what one person believes about another, as running estimates with a weight of evidence, read with a confidence (`estimate`, `impressionOf`; `believedValue` pulls the value to the prior by its uncertainty). Keys: state cues `fatigue`, `pain`, `fear`, `mood`; `fear@<placeId>`; `trait:<hexaco>`; `tie:<otherId>`; `trust:<voiceId>`; `skill:<skillId>` (how good the target is at a craft: written only when the host calls `hear` with a `skill:<id>` key). The host reports observations: `glimpseOf(observer, target, at, clarity?)` reads the target's outward signs (perceived state reduced by their reserve; a lost moving capacity shows as a limp through any reserve), `observeAct` an act (a place avoided or approached, someone gone to, a voice heeded or refused, tags such as `flee`, `steady`, `help` as trait evidence), `hear` testimony (`selfReport(target)` is the target's own words, which hide `wordsReserve` × more), `acquaintWith` years of history (traits and public family ties). Clarity scales both weight and error; the error is a hash of (observer, target, key, minute), so no RNG is drawn. Weight decays by kind (state 6 h, place fear 21 days, ties 90, trust 30, skill 365; traits never). `setReserve(p, { pain })` sets how much a person keeps from showing (default `0.1 + 0.4 × (1 − emotionality)` for pain and fear). Decisions read it in two places: cognition's `companion:<id>` term on a risky offer whose `with` names people the decider holds impressions of (`companionSteadiness`: believed calm and unhurt helps, frightened or hurt hurts), and `predictAs` / `previewCommandAs`, which run `predict` / `previewCommand` on `imagine(observer, target)`: a copy with traits, fear, pain, fatigue, mood, ties and voice trust replaced by the observer's beliefs and private memory dropped. A host shows the player's read with the same call it uses for one villager judging another. Bounded: 32 impressions, 48 cues each. Without an observation call no state is written.
- **Direct control (1.6.0).** A host can take direct control of a person with `command(p, { voiceId, affordanceId | action, since, repeat? })`, or under `stepCommunity` with `StepOptions.controlled` (authoritative when given: entries are put in force, absent people are released). This is a separate channel from suggestions, not a stronger suggestion: suggestion and insist semantics are unchanged. While a command holds, the person does the commanded offer whatever they would have chosen; the record's verdict is `commanded` with `margin` (how far their own choice out-scored it), and other voices are refused `cannot`/`commanded` without counters. It is suspended for one decision, then resumes, when the target is not offered, the person cannot (asleep, capacity, skill) or a pressing bodily need comes first. It ends (`will.lastCommand.reason`) at death, a mental break, being downed, or an act the person will not do (a held prohibition or a closing obligatory duty: no order makes a firmly convinced person break a norm). A one-job command ends `done` when its activity completes; `repeat: true` keeps it. The price: autonomy once when first obeyed and per controlled hour, voice pressure per hour, trust per hour scaled by the margin, a memory of being commanded, and trust learning as for compliance (no credit when it goes well, the faster loss when not). `previewCommand(p, affordances, cmd)` reads whether it would hold and the margin without changing anything. `releaseCommand(p, reason?)` ends it.

## What stays with the host

HF models the person. The host owns everything else, by design rather than as a gap: space, maps, pathfinding and travel (folded into `Affordance.duration`); inventory, money and jobs; job reservation and contention between people claiming the same work; work priorities (expressible as goals or standing suggestions); factions and who belongs to which group (`joinGroups` takes the host's names); combat resolution, weapons and hit rolls (HF decides fight, flee or comply at each interrupt and owns injuries, bleeding and downing); world events and their RNG. A RimWorld-like colony was the test case for this boundary (gap analysis, 2026-10-04): its five blocking gaps (direct control, mental breaks, injury depth, insiders and outsiders, save migration) were closed in engine 1.6.0 and are covered above. Cost at that scale: HF does nothing between a person's events, `decide` grows linearly with the number of offers (about 26 µs at 14 offers, 455 µs at 300, measured 2026-10-04), and the host's `affordancesFor` (pathfinding per offer) is expected to dominate. Current timings are in `npm run bench`.

For concrete offer/outcome boundaries, see [expected benefits and observed outcomes](../packages/human/README.md#expected-benefits-and-observed-outcomes). Advertised recovery, promised payment and possible company are not evidence that those effects occurred.

## Locked decisions (2026-10-02, after the early game-design review)

- No single willpower fuel. Acting against impulse comes from competing terms, real fatigue cost, habits and precommitments.
- Decisions read the **perceived** body; performance and health use the true body. Chronic sleep loss is under-perceived.
- Traits only scale terms (coefficients). They never branch logic.
- Interrupts: the host processes `activity.reviewAt` by calling `decide`, then `reviewed` for continuation or reconciling an interruption before beginning another activity. Awake reviews are normally at most 30 minutes apart; sleep has a longer cadence, capped by body thresholds and pending-commitment wake reviews. `tick` alone does not dispatch the host's decision loop. See [scheduled activity reviews](../packages/human/README.md#scheduled-activity-reviews).
- Hosts must offer zero-prerequisite floor affordances (rest in place, wait). Tests check that nobody starves while food is reachable.
- Learned expectations deviate from advertisements by a clamped step per outcome. The trace shows both.
- Narration cites recalled episodes when memory changed a choice.
- Saves: snapshot JSON with `schema` + `engine`, plus input-log replay (seed + suggestions + host events).
- Games run the simulation in a Web Worker. React renders snapshots only.
- Determinism: affordances are processed in stable id order; ties break by (utility, id). `Math.random` and `Date.now` are banned in `packages/human`, and so are the platform's transcendental functions (`Math.exp`, `Math.log`, `Math.cos`, `**`, …): engine code uses `dexp`, `dlog`, `dcos` and `dpow` from `core/libm.ts`, which give the same bits on every machine (test/libm.test.ts scans the source and pins golden outputs).
- Benchmark: 20 people for 30 simulated days in under 2 s headless.
- No theological names for mechanisms.

## Determinism and saves

- All randomness comes from `person.rng`, or from host-owned RNG for world events. Given the same seed and inputs, the result is byte-identical when the host's calls fall on the same minutes, on any OS and CPU architecture. Discrete events (decisions, missed commitments, goals) do not depend on how a host chunks `tick` calls; continuous state agrees to floating-point rounding (~1e-9) across different chunkings, and is byte-identical when calls fall on the 60-minute `tick` grid.
- `Person` is plain JSON. `snapshot(p)` returns a deep clone, and `restore(json)` validates `schema` and fills defaults. Both live in `restore.ts`, which checks each optional slice with its owning module's internal `sanitize*` function (listed in the module table above).
- A save is the people's snapshots, the community's host-side state (`communityState(c)`) and the world's state. Resume with `createCommunity(people, saved)` and the world's `state` option (`createVillage` takes one). Restoring only the people and calling `createCommunity(people)` diverges: day hooks run twice and queued advice and standing-advice completions are lost.
- A host with its own norm catalog sets `World.catalog`; `stepCommunity` passes it to `finish`, which reads make-ups owed for a break under necessity from it (default `DEFAULT_NORMS`). A norm missing from the catalog is not excused.
- Bounded collections: episodes ≤ 200, beliefs ≤ 300, impressions ≤ 32 with ≤ 48 cues each, trace ≤ 32 (fewer with `setRetention`), gists ≤ 64, year records ≤ 150, day records ≤ 120 (and by age with `setRetention`), emotions ≤ 12, breaches ≤ 50, intentions ≤ 50, voice history ≤ 5 per voice. Breach, injury and illness ids come from counters (`nextBreach`, `body.nextId`), so they stay unique after eviction. `restore` holds a saved person to the same bounds and refuses a non-finite `now` or one beyond `MAX_MINUTE` (2.0.0, security review H2).
- Versions: the HF release version is the package version and `FRAMEWORK_VERSION` (semver, tagged `vX.Y.Z`, notes in the root CHANGELOG.md). `ENGINE_VERSION` versions behaviour and the save format separately and is named in each release's notes. ## Scope notes

Parameters are engineering defaults chosen for plausible behavior at game time scales. Where a published finding informs a shape, the module's doc comment names it: the two-process sleep model, the power-law practice curve, exponential forgetting, OCC appraisal, HEXACO, Schwartz values. The parameters are not calibrated predictions of human behavior. Each module states in one paragraph what it covers and what it does not.

### Demonstrated methods

A host reports observations actually received by a learner with `receiveMethodCues(p, {contextId, cues})`. Cues are categorical `{cue, value}` strings; a missing cue is unknown, never a negative condition. The current context replaces the previous one and expires after 120 minutes; clear it on task changes. A demonstration requires the same context, all explained conditions already received, an action, source, observed success/failure and a stable `demonstrationId`. The last 256 acquired example ids remain protected across rule/evidence eviction; once an id leaves both that horizon and retained rule evidence, it can be admitted again. Hosts must not report hidden world facts as received observations.

The optional `Person.methods` stores acquired conditions and bounded evidence, independent of scalar skills. A held-out object's new context may contain the same observed cue structure. `methodEvidence(p, action, contextId)` queries that evidence; cognition uses that same reader for offers whose `methodContext` matches. Terms name the method and source (`method:<id>:<sourceId>`). Attention, source trust, failed outcomes and disuse grade support for each action independently; successful alternatives do not count as failures; missing or mismatching required cues yield no matching rule. An unobserved hidden exception can still match the received cues; the framework does not infer it. In the routing control, lower-effort help is an ordinary safe fallback; this does not demonstrate learned exception detection. Ordinary needs, norms, willingness and vetoes still operate. The coefficients are engineering defaults, not calibrated predictions.

Limits: 24 rules, 8 conditions each, 16 retained examples per rule, 256 recent example ids, 24 current cues; identifiers 96 characters and values 128. Restore drops malformed/unsafe entries and unknown fields. This is conditional action acquisition, not a universal planner, inferred hidden diagnosis or qualification. The domain-neutral [headless routing control](../packages/human/examples/method-control.ts) keeps two methods, one exception and identical host offers/outcomes across trained, altered-valid-teaching, withheld, scalar-skill-only and acquired-state-ablation arms.

- `ENGINE_VERSION` 2.1.0 (2026-10-10, release 2.2.0) adds optional `Person.methods` and `Affordance.methodContext`. The 2.0.0 → 2.1.0 migration stamps the version; no method state is invented. Existing hosts without acquired method state retain their prior decisions. Old continuation digest controls remove the engine stamp and continue to pin behavior.

- `ENGINE_VERSION` 2.2.0 (2026-10-14, release 2.2.1) grades each acquired action independently. Success of another action no longer counts as a failure. The 2.1.0 → 2.2.0 migration stamps the version and preserves optional method records. Same-action failure, source trust, decay and restore bounds remain unchanged.


### Received consequences (2.3.0)

`prospection/` owns optional `Person.prospection`; its entries retain source, `told` or host-seeded `known` channel, received time and any own completed-trigger time. `Percept.consequences` carries typed `ReceivedConsequence` entries; `withdrawConsequences` carries source-local id/revision withdrawals. Both are acquired only after attention from an external `actorId`, addressed to this person or broadcast with no target. Self testimony and testimony explicitly addressed to another recipient are rejected. Invalid or conflicting combined batches mutate no prospection state. No direct observation channel, host utility value or invented goal progress is admitted. Known setup claims are described below.

A trigger matches action, target and place exactly, including omitted values. Without planning, a route contains one current action and one described successor. The successor starts after the current action and `availableFrom`, and completes no later than `availableUntil`. That window is separate from a goal's `deadline`, which remains urgency. The host owns physical successor offers and another person's actual consent.

`agenda/` owns optional `Goal.temporal: { halfLifeMinutes }`. A finite positive half-life opts the goal into existing base value × action success × deterministic completion-time discount. This applies to both direct and received routes. Successor goal matching uses `Goal.advancedBy` only; a current offer's `Affordance.advances` hint is not inherited by its described successor. For a received route, access probability is current-action success × stated chance × confidence × live source trust; terminal success adds the existing successor success estimate. The unknown-action success prior remains 0.9. Knowledge ablations retain temporal goals; temporal-off is a separate time-and-success-valuation control.

`cognition/` reads one whole route at most. Each goal receives the greater of its direct and indirect contribution, rather than their sum. The route pays one expected successor effort/material cost using the same pure readers as actual offers. That cost is undiscounted and unscaled by maturity. Route ranking uses the rounded units actually emitted in decisions. Ordinary needs, norms, habits and will remain active. A completed own trigger consumes its opportunity without access, consent or terminal goal progress; only actual outcomes advance goals through existing agenda rules.

Bounds: four additions/withdrawals per percept, 16 entries, 64 revision records, 96-character identifiers and a seven-day horizon. Equal/lower revisions do not amplify or refresh. Higher revisions replace; withdrawals leave bounded revision memory, including unseen ids. Consumed entries are evicted before unused entries; remaining eviction order is deterministic by age, source and id, while a batch that would exceed still-valid revision capacity is rejected atomically. `consequenceEvidence` exposes retained entries, current feasibility and retained revisions without mutation or RNG; it is not a durable rejection audit. Capacity is global, not allocated by trust or source: a prolific source can occupy it and block new ids until revision space expires. This release adds no source-fairness policy; hosts bound delivered premises. Restore drops malformed optional data and preserves valid provenance and consumption. The 2.2.0 → 2.3.0 engine migration stamps the version and invents no state.

The [domain-neutral control](../packages/human/examples/received-consequences.ts) records favorable and unfavorable cases, matched knowledge/goal controls, refusal and a renamed second domain. Without planning this is bounded two-step valuation. It does not search the host world, model future bodies or waiting opportunity cost, execute plans, synthesize goals or guarantee outcomes. Parameters remain engineering assumptions. Existing hosts without temporal goals retain their prior behavior.

### Bounded planning (opt-in)

`enablePlanning(p, { depth?, presentBias? })` or `PersonSpec.enable.planning` creates `p.planning`, owned by `prospection/`. Depth counts the current offer and its successors: integer 2..5, default 4. The search links retained claims by exact action, target and place, never repeats an entry, and explores depth first in stable `(sourceId, id)` order. It stops after 64 feasible node expansions. Each successor waits until its window opens and must finish within it. Access multiplies action success, chance, confidence and live source trust at every link; each temporal goal takes its greatest direct or chain-node value. One whole route wins against the direct baseline in rounded decision units. Its cost sums expected nonpositive successor effort/material costs. Optional `presentBias` (β in (0, 1]) scales successor value and cost once; direct value stays unchanged. A chosen route with multiple successors adds its entry ids joined by `>` as the cost term's `ref`.

`seedConsequences(p, sourceId, claims)` records prior world knowledge as `known`, without attention or source trust. It rejects the person's own source id and shares told claims' validation, revision memory and expiry. With planning, both acquisition paths accept batches of 16 and retain 64 entries; without it, the existing 4/16 limits remain. Revision memory stays capped at 64. Restore validates planning before admitting the larger retained set. `planRoute(p, aff, now?)` returns the selected successor actions, start/end times, cumulative access and per-goal values, or `undefined` when no route improves on direct value. It uses ordinary scoring costs without host scarcity, reads held state at the requested time, and neither mutates state nor consumes RNG.

The [control](../packages/human/examples/planning-control.ts) completes a four-step task in two renamed domains with depth 4; reactive and depth-3 arms never start that task. A β=0.5 arm chooses a smaller two-step project with an immediate preparation benefit first. This is bounded search over retained claims, not a general planner: it does not search the host's world, infer procedures, execute plans or model another person's agency. Existing hosts without the planning slice retain single-successor behavior exactly. Scientific shapes and engineering assumptions are cited in `prospection/` SCOPE and [research/curiosity-and-play.md](../research/curiosity-and-play.md) §Planning.

### Projects from what is seen (opt-in)

Newly enabled projects (`behaviour: 2`) credit new perceived highs via `Goal.project.peak`; re-enabling seeds active perceived-progress goals from current progress, while unmarked restored goals keep 2.14.0 crediting.

`enableProjects(p, spec?)` or `PersonSpec.enable.projects` creates `p.projects`, owned by `projects/`. An attended `Percept.prospect` names a host-shown opportunity (`make`, `arrange`, `gather`, `finish`), with optional place, materials and perceived progress. The newest observation per id wins; eight prospects are retained, least recently seen evicted first with stable ties, and forgotten after one day by default. Identifiers and labels are bounded to 512 characters; material lists to eight. Hosts must use a new id for a distinct opportunity.

On every tick segment, including minute-sized host steps, a relaxed, awake person may adopt one prospect as an agenda goal serving competence and leisure. Physiological or safety urgency and fear each close the gate at 0.35; open commitment windows take priority. Appeal combines relaxed needs, openness (conscientiousness for finishing), leisure/competence/autonomy urgency, a small curiosity bonus and an unfinished-task resumption bonus. Ties use prospect id. Defaults allow adoption at most every 30 minutes, with a 60-minute cooldown after abandonment. No progress rise for 180 minutes abandons the project. Eight terminal history entries support narration; the agenda remains the goal owner. All coefficients are engineering defaults, not fitted human estimates; scientific shapes are cited in the module SCOPE and [research/projects-and-persistence.md](../research/projects-and-persistence.md).

Restore recovers ownership from active project goals. If several exist, it keeps the newest adopted,
breaking ties by ascending goal id, and abandons the others through agenda with history and cooldown.
With none active, it clears ownership. Stale abandonment records history and cooldown even without
the ownership pointer. Reconciliation uses no RNG.

A project has a temporal half-life of 120 minutes so planning can value a route to it in free time. `Affordance.advances`, `Outcome.advances` and the bounded optional `ReceivedConsequence.successor.advances` can name either its goal id or prospect id. For a project adopted with perceived progress, completed contributions at or above the retained high are returned as `advanced` for outcome credit but do not change progress or its last-rise timestamp (unmarked legacy goals credit every contribution). Only an attended progress reading sets progress (including a decrease); 1 achieves it. For projects without perceived progress, ordinary events advance it. Completion marks the retained opportunity complete until fresh host evidence replaces it. The [control](../packages/human/examples/projects-control.ts) offers rest, wandering, a familiar pleasant pastime and a two-step fetch/place route for one day: projects on completes five placements; off and hungry arms adopt nothing. A second 180-minute scenario offers a three-step go/take/place route with depth-3 planning and curiosity, beside a pleasant familiar pastime and a fresh unfamiliar option every minute. It compares projects+persistence, projects only, persistence only and neither, counting adoption, placements, completed chains and chains unfinished within 20 minutes. An interruption arm becomes hungry at minute 60; it reports time to its first meal and placements afterward. Needs are host-maintained otherwise. These are deterministic host premises, not fitted human estimates. The slice invents no prospects, interprets no task, searches no world and provides no persistence or shielding. With the slice absent, even prospect-bearing percepts leave full-state digests unchanged.

### Promptings and turning away (2.4.0)

The first slice of [research/reconciliation.md](../research/reconciliation.md) §6 makes the inner event before assent a thing: a *prompt* is a noticed pull (habit, emotion, need, or a host `temptation` tag) toward an option that a held norm or goal opposes. `promptings/` owns optional `Person.promptings`, opt-in and RNG-free. `consider()` adds one bounded `promptings:turn` term (−`turnMax` × practice) to an option carrying a prompt when the person has a practised response, never at survival desperation. After `decide`, the composite records each prompt as `assented`, `turned` or `passed` (a review that keeps the running activity records nothing), and practice grows with diminishing gains and halves over 60 days of disuse. A prompt is never a breach or an intention: only an assented, finished act reaches `conscience/ recordDeed`.

Each person holds a reading of their own inner pulls (`whisper`, `inclination`, `intrusion`, `fault`, `none`) with provenance. The first three and `none` share mechanics and differ only in vocabulary. `fault`, the scrupulous reading, adds a small guilt emotion at the prompt (an emotion, never a breach); with a host-set `echoRate` it also gains a decaying recurrence pull, a labelled assumption from contested evidence, off by default. Lenses are read-only: `promptingsReading(p)` summarises recent prompts and practice; `nafsReading(p)` names the three Qurʾanic descriptions (12:53, 75:2, 89:27) with the evidence that matches each, never a level or a rank. No qalb, nafs, fitra or ʿaql scalar is stored (research/decisions.md, 2026-10-18).

The [headless control](../packages/human/examples/promptings-control.ts) lives five people for 60 days on the same cue: no response (assents daily), a practised redirect (assents the first 12 days, then never, prompts flat), the `fault` reading (the same choices and breaches as no response, plus guilt at each prompt) and a fixed penalty at two strengths (a step, always or never, with no curve and no prompt record). Persons without the slice continue exactly: full-state digests match npm 2.3.0 with only the engine stamp normalized.

### Bodily forecast (2.5.0)

A person can anticipate their own body during exertion they know must follow an act: "I can reach the top, but I'll be spent for the descent." The host declares the follow-on on the offer, `Affordance.followedBy: FollowOn[]` (label, duration, effort, focus). `body/ forecastBody` simulates a copy of the body through the act and those spans with the ordinary body model and default rates, read as the person perceives it; `prospection/forecast.ts` turns the worst perceived fatigue, sleepiness, hunger or thirst during the follow-on spans into one bounded term, `forecast:body` = −1.2 × smoothstep(0.8, 0.97, worst). It is pure and RNG-free. `bodyForecast(p, aff)` returns the spans and a first-person line for narration. Offers without `followedBy` get no forecast, so existing hosts continue exactly.

Limits come from the body model: exertion relaxes toward the current effort; only a person with the opt-in fatigue store (2.6.0, `body.depletion`) carries a long ascent into the descent. An open fast's hunger damping applies (thirst is not damped). Unfelt sleep debt stays unfelt, host surroundings are not applied, and nothing plans rests on the way. The [control](../packages/human/examples/body-forecast-control.ts) shows a rested person taking the far point, a depleted person taking the near one, the same depleted person going far when the return is not declared (ablation), a fed person at 20:00 weighing a harder return, and identical results in a renamed domain.


### Evening review, muhasaba (2.7.0)

The second slice of [research/reconciliation.md](../research/reconciliation.md) §7: once a day, from `at` (default 21:00) or at the day's close if the evening passed, a person with `enableReview` looks over the commitments the chronicle recorded as kept or broken since their last review. Each broken one (never a released or excused one: 2:286) forms or renews a *resolve*, an if–then intention for its next occasion (E1, Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006). While a matching commitment is pending, `consider()` adds one bounded `review:resolve` term (each resolve once per option, clamped to ±0.3) with the sign of that commitment's own term, at most `resolveScale` (0.3) × strength. Strength gains half the remaining headroom per lapse, keeps 60% when the commitment is kept, and halves over seven days. Held styles: `accounting` (Q 59:18, where a daily reading of "tomorrow" is an inference; ʿUmar's saying reported by Tirmidhi after 2459, an athar) forms full resolves and feels nothing extra (an engineering mapping to E3, self-compassion after failure); `ruminating` forms weak resolves and feels some distress (E4, abstract repetitive thought); `none` never reviews. `reviewLine(p)` gives the person's words: "I didn't keep the evening task. Next time I'll be ready when it comes."

Limits: the review reads commitments only, not methods or promptings; it models no tawba conditions, divine reckoning or acceptance, and no effect on sleep onset. The [control](../packages/human/examples/review-control.ts) shows a person without review lingering all 30 days, the accounting reviewer settling into the task after about twelve days of alternation (23 of 30 kept, 10 of them decided by the resolve; then the task's own habit carries it), the ruminating reviewer lingering throughout with more distress (its slower resolve, 0.65 by day 30, never tips the choice), and a person who never lapses with no resolve formed.

### Game requests: going along, fullness, a voice's word (2.8.0)

From the games' requests ([triage](game-requests-2026-10-18.md)). Each piece is opt-in or new API, so a host that uses none of it continues exactly (full-state digests match npm 2.3.0 with only the engine stamp normalized).
- **Going along.** `Affordance.alongside: PersonId[]` names people going this way (unlike `with`, who take part). For each known, living tie in it, `consider()` adds `together:<id>` = 0.12 × closeness. `decide(p, offers, { reconsider: true })` drops the running activity's inertia bonus for that one decision, for a host-forced review after something changed (a tug, a call); promise inertia stays. In the [control](../packages/human/examples/game-requests-control.ts) a person resting beside a close partner goes on with them in 12 of 12 seeds only with both; either alone leaves 0 of 12.
- **Fullness.** `enableFullness(p)` or `enable: { fullness: true }`: an offer advertising food, water or relief to a need already past its comfort level gets `full:<need>` = −0.03 × gain × over² (negative alliesthesia). Idle choice then falls through to waiting or sitting: 120 sips in eight full hours fall to 2. HF's `relief` is pain relief; HF has no bladder.
- **A voice's word.** `seedVoice(p, voiceId, trust, { replace? })` sets an authored starting trust on an existing person. `noteVoicePromise(p, voiceId, { kept, at, action })` moves trust by whether the voice did what it said: kept gains 0.1 of the headroom, divided by 1 + the decayed count of kept promises for that action; broken loses 0.25 of the trust.
- **Forecast thresholds per offer.** `Affordance.forecastAt: { comfortAt, spentAt }` replaces the 2.5.0 forecast's 0.8 and 0.97 for that offer, for people who live high on a readout (night keepers).

### Game requests: several commitments, cued responses, a resolve's name (2.9.0)

From the polish requests filed against 2.8.0 ([triage](game-requests-2026-10-18.md#outcome-hf-290-2026-10-19)). Scope: four small generic changes and three doc notes; no new faculty. Each is opt-in or additive except the first, which changes only outcomes that name two or more commitments. It does not cover: a per-response half-life (no source gives phrases different decay), a separate `reviewLabel` (labels are documented as noun phrases instead), negative goal terms (goals stay attractors by design), or revising a response in review (roadmap §3b).
- **Several named commitments.** When `Outcome.fulfills` (default: the activity's `Affordance.fulfills`) lists two or more ids, every listed commitment that is pending and in window is kept, and no other. With one listed (or none of several eligible), one completion keeps the earliest-ending match, explicit or by action, as before, so a single listed commitment can still lose to an earlier implicit match. In the [control](../packages/human/examples/game-requests-29-control.ts) one dinner keeps a word to a friend and the daily call.
- **Cued responses.** `PromptingResponse.cue`: an action, an offer tag, or the opposing norm or goal. At a prompt the person uses the most practised response whose cue matches, else the most practised uncued one; a cued response never serves another prompt (the literal "global best" fallback would let a phrase learned at the alarm turn the person from gossip, the case the request reports). Without cues this is the 2.4.0 rule exactly. Each response used at a decision point gains practice once.
- **Practice half-life.** `enablePromptings(p, { practiceHalfLifeDays })`, one value per person (the need is the host's time scale), default 60 days. Practice decays lazily from last use, so a new half-life applies to disuse already elapsed: a week unused leaves 0.80 at 0.74 by default, 0.40 at seven days.
- **A resolve's name.** `Term.ref` carries the resolve key on `review:resolve` when exactly one resolve weighed in; `source` stays `review:resolve` so exact matches keep working. Review users' traces gain the field; their decisions are unchanged.
- **Doc notes.** Commitment labels should be noun phrases for `reviewLine`; a recurring commitment lapses on a day the host does not live; goal terms are never negative.

## Word and witness (2.10.0)

### Belief provenance and contradiction

`beliefs/` owns optional `Belief.provenance` and `memory.contradictions`. An attended claim carrying `origin` or `lineage` records its first known origin, the direct teller (`firstFrom`), the acquisition minute and an optional opaque host token of at most 64 characters. First acquisition wins. `confirm` resets source accounts but preserves this metadata; belief eviction drops it. `provenanceOf(p, prop)` reads it. Metadata never affects credence or decisions. Restore drops malformed provenance and removes over-long lineage tokens without truncating them; `perceive` likewise records an over-long lineage as absent (origin and teller are kept) rather than throwing, so a host that needs the token must keep it within 64 characters.

`converse(speaker, listener, { at, provenance: true })` carries origin and lineage on its host-visible claims and testimony percepts, including advice. A speaker without provenance names themself as origin. Relays keep the token unchanged; the host owns the graph and disclosure. With the flag absent or false, output stays unchanged. Repetition and the suppression of news already held compare propositions and values, never lineage.

`contradicted(p, { sourceId, prop, value, reporterId?, at? })` requires a retained account from that same source on that proposition with the opposite value. Two independent sources disagreeing is ordinary uncertainty and costs neither source trust. A missing account returns `no-account`; a matching account returns `same-account`; self returns `self`. A previously noted source/proposition pair returns `already-noted` without changing anything, even after confirmation clears the original account.

An applied contradiction changes only `memory.sourceTrust[sourceId]`: `after = before - 0.25 * weight * before`. Weight is 1 when the person heard both accounts directly. For a third-party report it is `testimonyWeight(trustOf(p, reporterId))`, so a distrusted reporter can frame less and a reporter at or below the 0.2 trust floor causes no loss. This never raises trust. A pair loses trust once, at the strongest evidence: a later, better-trusted report of the same pair applies only the difference, so the total is `1 - 0.25 * strongest weight` whatever the order, and the record keeps the weight applied. A reporter at or below the trust floor (weight 0) returns `untrusted-reporter` and records nothing, so a distrusted ally cannot use up the pair and shield the source from a later first-hand contradiction. Multiplicative losses commute, subject to floating-point precision, while reporter weights stay fixed. Reports delivered in one percept share one basis: `perceive` reads the reporter's trust once, before any of them applies, so their order within the percept does not matter. Across separate deliveries order can matter when a contradiction lowers the trust of someone who later reports, as later evidence from a discredited reporter should weigh less. The loss coefficient is an engineering default, equal to the existing loss on confirmed falsehood. Contradiction changes no belief, relationship trust or voice trust and does not establish deceit.

The host can deliver up to four `reports: [{ sourceId, prop, value }]` in one attended `told` percept whose `actorId` names the reporter. `perceive` checks each against the listener's own source accounts. It does not deliver the report to an absent person or infer a contradiction merely because world truth changed. The newest 32 contradiction records are kept by event minute; deduplication lasts while that bounded record remains. Restore validates, deduplicates and bounds this optional store. Calls without the new data write no optional fields.

### Believed regard

`social/impressions.ts` owns the opt-in `regard` cue: how I believe another person regards me, from −1 to 1, with prior 0 and evidence half-life 60 days. `regardOf(p, otherId, now)` returns the estimate and confidence without mutation. `noteKnownBy(p, { otherId, act, via, sourceId?, at? })` records my evidence that the other knows of my act. `via: 'seen'` has weight 1; `via: 'told'` uses information-source trust in `sourceId`, and writes nothing without a source or when its testimony weight is zero. An attended own-act percept (`actorId === p.id`) names the people I saw see it in `witnesses`. A secret witness or an unknown relay supplies no evidence.

The sample projects my own understanding: the act's explicit valence, or defaults (`promise-broken` −0.6, `promise-kept` +0.3, `harm` −0.6, `help` +0.4, `apology` +0.2, otherwise 0), counts in full for its target and at half strength for a third party. My self-judgement under my held norms adds 0.3 × praiseworthiness. The sample is clamped to −1..1; negative samples carry twice the evidence weight. These coefficients are engineering defaults. HF does not read the other's actual view or infer undisclosed knowledge.

`socialTerms` adds `regard:<id>` only for a target with a negative regard cue on an offer tagged `repair` or with action `apology`/`amends`: `min(0.3, 0.5 × (−mean) × confidence × (0.5 + agreeableness))`, rounded like other terms. The term never applies to someone held as deceased. Without a regard cue no term is added, even if other impression cues exist. This can change a close choice; it guarantees neither repair nor forgiveness. The term persists while the regard cue stays negative: HF does not decide whether an apology was accepted, so the host delivers that feedback (a reply, a changed relationship, `noteKnownBy` with a positive act) or the person keeps preferring repair. In the control, with no such feedback, A apologises every day. `regard` is a reserved impression cue key from 2.10.0; a host that stored its own cue under that key should rename it.

For a broken promise, the host delivers each person's evidence separately:

| Person / store | Delivery |
|---|---|
| Breaker's own commitment record | The composite's internal `recordMissed` records the lapse when `tick` or `finish` finds the broken commitment; it sends no percept to anyone else. |
| Promisee's relationship with the breaker | Deliver a `promise-broken` percept with `actorId` = breaker and `targetId` = promisee. Its relationship changes only after that percept is attended. |
| Witnesses' judgement of the breaker | Include `norms` on the percept each witness receives; each judges under their own held norms. |
| Breaker's believed regard | Deliver the breaker's own-act percept with `witnesses` naming only people the breaker saw see it; use `noteKnownBy` for a subsequently learned relay. |

Relationships (`social.relationships`) and impressions (`social.impressions`) are separate stores. The promisee's actual trust in the breaker is a relationship value; the breaker's belief about the promisee's view is an impression cue. Recording one does not copy it into the other.
