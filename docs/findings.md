- **2026-10-19 — Playtester calibration: no boredom, and one hard night ended every session.** In-site level-ups reported three things. All 16 HF runs of The Night Watch quit from distress in winter. Colony's commuter and restless quit on Day 1 after streams of "not now". Every game sat at engagement 0.7–0.9, with lulls of 0 s and no one looking away. The harness caused most of this, though not all of it. (1) _Bad news weighed too much, because HF holds it for hours._ Each game event was appraised into HF distress, whose half-life is 3 hours. Stopping always carried HF's `rest` tag, and distress favours `rest`. A toy garden with two heavy blows at minute 6 kept distress at 0.44–0.59 for the rest of the session. Every base persona quit when the 20-minute stretch of play lapsed, including the completionist, who finishes the plain garden. HF also keeps joy and distress apart: a merge only raises the matching emotion. So a session of mixed nights raises both. Measured on The Night Watch, the completionist's distress and joy were 0.85 and 0.88 at minute 15. (2) _The harness could not produce boredom on a busy screen._ An event without stakes still added 0.4 × salience to engagement. A drip of log lines and character movements therefore held engagement above the boredom line of 0.35. A toy that only ticks (valence 0, every beat) bored 2 of 8 personas; the rest left on their agenda.

  _Changes, all in `session.ts`:_
  - An event's pull on attention is now 0.12 + 0.88 × |valence| (it was 0.4 + 0.6 × |valence|).
  - Events below |valence| 0.2 count at half weight as a reward or a setback.
  - A minute is hard when its setbacks reach 0.15 and outweigh its rewards. Stopping carries `rest` only when 3 of the last 5 minutes were hard and the window's setbacks exceed 1.5 × its rewards. So frustration means a clearly losing stream.
  - A single event at valence −0.4 or worse is a new `setback` moment: a loss the player plays through. This removes the early-quit path. Even a catastrophe that does not end the game is played through for at least 5 minutes, whatever the distress.
  - The mischief-maker is the exception on the refusing toy, because its pokes are answered. It is never frustrated there.

  _Toys (tested):_
  - The ticker now bores out at least half of the base sessions.
  - The hard night is played through with no frustrated moment.
  - The refusing keeper still drives every other persona off. So does a keeper whose refusals are mild (valence −0.15), which guards against small setbacks going unseen.
  - Side effect: the returner is now about even on coming back to a plain garden with nothing left open (play-on 0.064 against elsewhere 0.066). The day-two test uses the cliffhanger toy instead.
  - The base-persona digest was re-pinned deliberately.

  _Real games, before → after (8 personas):_
  - The Night Watch (2 seeds, through the site's own harness at af0bfde): 16 of 16 quit from distress at 3–20 minutes, mostly in winter 1. After: 14 of 16 at 5–35 minutes, most in winters 2–3. One session stopped for leisure, one for the agenda, and the personas looked away in 5 sessions (was 0).
  - The Pass (3 seeds; the returner's evenings make the session counts differ): frustrated sessions 24 of 33 → 10 of 26, still from walkouts and refusals. Engagement fell by 0.04–0.10.
  - The Glance: frustrated 11 of 27 → 0 of 26. Eight sessions now put it down bored (was 0), mostly at N1–N2, which matches LLM testers' reports of dead air (CALIBRATION-NOTES C1).
  - The Two Voices: frustrated 21 of 24 → 0 of 24. Every session now plays to Saturday, still with no boredom.

  _What is left (assumed, not confirmed):_
  - The remaining Night Watch quits come from windows the adapter scores as clearly lost: a wolf getting in is −0.55 and lost sacks reach −0.8, against +0.45 for a wolf driven off. The personas do lose grain every night. Whether a good player like the owner does is unknown. That is the adapter's scoring and the game's balance, not the harness.
  - Colony's adapter was not run here. A Day 1 quit there after a stream of refusals is frustration working as designed, unless the adapter scores reasoned refusals as setbacks (CALIBRATION-NOTES C6).
  - The structural limit is in HF: game events enter life-scale emotions. A session-scale affect store, or an HF appraisal channel for fiction, would remove it (see the roadmap).
  - The boredom fix lets a busy screen bore, but The Pass and The Two Voices still bore almost nobody. C1's proposal, boredom measured from the last event that answered an input or changed the scene, was not tried.
