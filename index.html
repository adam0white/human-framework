    How the models differ

    Full action loop: with its components enabled, the policy weighs goal-valued contribution, deadline opportunity, effort, perceived conditions, recovery, information gathering, assistance and recognized promises. It is a reactive scorer with deadline awareness; it does not search a multistep plan.

    Simple utility baseline: work is ranked using its output and the person's current task proficiency; rest, food, observation and help receive fixed scores. It does not adapt its scoring to strain, beliefs or promises.

    Planned simple recovery: every actor eats at estimated hunger 60% when food is available, otherwise rests at fatigue 65%, otherwise follows fixed work preferences. Recovery priorities and perceived capacity can take precedence over a numeric score; the inspector shows those tiers.

    All use the same actions and world rules. Your request and reason stay recorded, but work or help that exceeds physical capacity is replaced by automatic recovery. The same limit applies to manual play and every policy, including when Body coupling is off.

    About this model, its assumptions & limitsRead the research boundaries

    Human Framework is a simulation inspired by the world as we currently understand it. This prototype uses authored, uncalibrated scenarios and coefficients. Running successfully is an engineering result; it does not establish that the model explains real people.

    Fatigue, hunger, proficiency, confidence and trust are bounded modeling quantities, not physiological or psychological measurements. A confidence value is not a calibrated probability that a belief is correct. Scores express policy preferences in arbitrary utility units. Completing a task does not measure a person's goodness or divine acceptance.

    The Islamic grounding begins from a Sunni, Hanafi–Maturidi orientation, while distinguishing revelation, interpretation, empirical findings, explanatory models and engineering choices. Commitments here are authored promises. Spiritual faculties, consciousness and divine decree are not numerical mechanics.

    The current scope is perception, effort, task-specific practice, promises and small shared tasks. Detailed physiology, genetics, lifespan development, clinical conditions and society-wide dynamics remain outside the prototype. No unsupported transfer between skills is added by these presets.

    Action cards show known task rules and estimates from the selected person's viewpoint. Success also depends on hidden conditions. Effort is the action's fatigue contribution; elapsed time adds its own body changes, and bounds can limit the final change. The researcher panel exposes hidden facts for inspection, and replay exports include the full scenario.

    All text is formatted from structured records. The simulation runs without an LLM or external service. Diagrams are illustrative; each round advances explicit simulated time. The same engine version, scenario, seed and command sequence reproduce a run. Shared seeds support comparison, but changed choices can still lead to different observations and consequences.
