## B13: cognitive training in an at-risk student population

Wong, Rosales and Looney (2024) studied 62 Southern California community-day-school students receiving six hours of computerized training. They report pre/post changes in working memory, task switching and processing speed, but explicitly lack a true control group. Their limitations also identify single measures for working memory/task switching and a need for longitudinal follow-up. This is evidence of observed task-test change in that sample, not a causal estimate of general-intelligence improvement or universal transfer. Access: methods, materials, discussion and limitations in the [full-text primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11351393/); raw data were not obtained. The original PubMed address returned no readable text in this pass.

**Decision:** retain as a limited training lead. It cannot calibrate the framework's practice rate or justify granting unrelated expertise. A future empirical test needs active controls, matched retesting and named transfer outcomes.

## Q29: skill decay in complex high-risk tasks

Klostermann, Conein, Felkl and Kluge's 2022 scoping review distinguishes task, training-method and person-related influences. Its discussion notes heterogeneous task definitions, possible overlapping source material and limited adaptive-transfer evidence. Access: bibliographic page, task/training passages and limitations in the [authors' institutional full-text copy](https://bibb-dspace.bibb.de/rest/bitstreams/0ebbbb41-752f-435b-ac0e-673dd25a5ed0/retrieve). The publisher URL returned 429. Included studies were not independently re-audited.

**Decision:** a useful map for choosing a bounded retention experiment, not a universal decay coefficient. Specify task, initial acquisition criterion, unused interval and refresher condition before comparing a retention model with no retention. Keep performance strain distinct from stored skill.
