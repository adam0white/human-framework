# Projects and persistence

2026-10-20. The sources behind two opt-in faculties in HF 2.14.0: self-set projects and goal persistence. Findings, models and HF's engineering choices are kept apart, as [source-method.md](source-method.md) requires. Every HF number is an engineering default. None is fitted to these studies.

**Access.** Records for Locke & Latham 2002, Gollwitzer 1999, Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, Shah et al. 2002, Altmann & Trafton 2002, Monsell 2003, Wrosch et al. 2003, Kurzban et al. 2013, Little 1983, Ovsiankina 1928, Zeigarnik 1927 and Ghibellini & Meier 2025 were checked by web search on 2026-10-20 (authors, year, venue, pages). Summaries come from abstracts and secondary pages, not full texts. Items marked *unverified* could not be confirmed. Do not quote wording from this note.

## 1. What each faculty claims

**Projects.** When a person's needs are met, they may turn what they perceive (materials, a place, an unfinished thing) into a self-set project. Temperament shapes the choice: openness raises the fit of a new project, conscientiousness raises the strength to finish it. Curiosity adds to the pull. A person holds one active project at a time. A project that stays stale is dropped.

**Persistence.** Once a person begins acting on a goal, a pursuit record gives a bonus to options that serve it. It adds a bounded switching cost to unrelated options. Bodily need urgency and fear scale both down. Commitments (duties) are never taxed. The pursuit releases after about 20 minutes unserved or about 4 hours in total.

## 2. Empirical findings

These paragraphs report what each source found. They do not say what HF does.

**Little 1983.** Little, B. R. (1983). Personal projects: A rationale and method for investigation. *Environment and Behavior*, 15(3), 273-309. Proposes the personal project as a unit of analysis for personality in its social, physical and temporal setting, and a method for eliciting and appraising projects. Later Personal Projects Analysis work groups appraisal dimensions (importance, enjoyment, control, progress, stress and others) into domains such as meaning, efficacy and stress. Well-being tends to go with meaningful, manageable projects and against stress (secondary summaries; for example McGregor & Little 1998, which I did not read). Book-length treatment: Little, Salmela-Aro & Phillips (eds.) 2007, *Personal Project Pursuit*, Erlbaum (catalogue only). Effect sizes: none retrieved.

**Locke & Latham 2002.** Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (2002). Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35-year odyssey. *American Psychologist*, 57(9), 705-717. A review of 35 years of goal-setting research. Specific, difficult goals raise performance over easy or "do your best" goals when the person is committed and has the ability and feedback. Effect sizes: not retrieved here (*unverified*).

**Ryan & Deci 2000.** Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. *American Psychologist*, 55(1), 68-78. A theory: autonomy, competence and relatedness support intrinsic motivation. Some parts, such as the reward-undermining effect, have mixed replication. Deci & Ryan's wider programme (1985 book; 2000 *Psychological Inquiry*) was not re-checked.

**Play and making.** Smilansky (1968), *The Effects of Sociodramatic Play on Disadvantaged Preschool Children*, Wiley, is the source cited for functional, constructive, dramatic play and games with rules. Secondary pages disagree on the third label, so this is *unverified* (catalogue and summaries only). Burghardt 2005, *The Genesis of Animal Play*, MIT Press: play is repeated, varied, not fully functional, voluntary, and starts when the animal is fed and safe (secondary sources only; already cited in [curiosity-and-play.md](curiosity-and-play.md)). Pellegrini's work on object play and tool use: I found the 2009 book *The Role of Play in Human Development* (Oxford University Press), whose chapters cover objects in exploration, play and tool use. I could not confirm any specific finding, and I could not find the 2007 *Developmental Review* article I had in mind (*unverified*). Gopnik 2020 (*Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 375, 20190502) is already in the sibling note. Nothing solid and checked links play to goal-directed construction beyond these framings.

**Zeigarnik 1927 and Ovsiankina 1928.** Zeigarnik, B. (1927). Über das Behalten von erledigten und unerledigten Handlungen. *Psychologische Forschung*, 9, 1-85. Interrupted tasks were recalled about twice as often as finished ones, on average, which she explained by a residual quasi-need. Ovsiankina, M. (1928). Die Wiederaufnahme unterbrochener Handlungen. *Psychologische Forschung*, 11, 302-379. Interrupted tasks were resumed in 115 of 141 cases across 28 participants (from a secondary review, Lindorfer 2012).

**Ghibellini & Meier 2025.** Ghibellini & Meier (2025); initials not confirmed. Interruption, recall and resumption: a meta-analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects. *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 12, DOI 10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w. The abstract as retrieved reports no memory advantage for unfinished tasks, which has been hard to replicate, and a general tendency to resume interrupted tasks. Effect sizes in secondary sources (recall ratio 0.99, d about 0.15, resumption about 67%) disagree on study counts and are *unverified*. The journal name is unverified: the search index labelled it Palgrave Communications, and I gave the current title of the DOI prefix 10.1057/s41599 (the journal was renamed from *Palgrave Communications* in 2020). I could not open the full text.

**Gollwitzer 1999; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006.** Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999). Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans. *American Psychologist*, 54(7), 493-503. If-then plans hand control of a goal-directed response to a situational cue. Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 38, 69-119. Reported d about 0.65 on goal attainment (confirmed from several secondary sources, not the chapter; counts of 94 tests or studies vary). The plans help with starting, with shielding an ongoing activity from interference, and with letting go.

**Shah, Friedman & Kruglanski 2002.** Shah, J. Y., Friedman, R., & Kruglanski, A. W. (2002). Forgetting all else: On the antecedents and consequences of goal shielding. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 83(6), 1261-1280. In six studies a focal goal suppressed the accessibility of competing goals. This was stronger with commitment, and weaker when the alternative goal was instrumental to the focal one. Shielding aided pursuit and attainment. Effect sizes: not retrieved.

**Monsell 2003.** Monsell, S. (2003). Task switching. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 7(3), 134-140. Switching tasks costs time and accuracy. Preparation shrinks the cost but does not remove it. The review attributes the cost to carry-over of the old task set and to reconfiguration time. These are laboratory trial costs in milliseconds. They are not minutes of everyday behaviour.

**Altmann & Trafton 2002.** Altmann, E. M., & Trafton, J. G. (2002). Memory for goals: An activation-based model. *Cognitive Science*, 26(1), 39-83. Goals are memory items whose activation decays and is refreshed by use and by priming, not entries on a stack. Fitted to Tower of Hanoi latency and error data. It covers interruption effects. A model; no single effect size.

**Klinger 1975.** Klinger, E. (1975). Consequences of commitment to and disengagement from incentives. *Psychological Review*, 82(1), 1-25. The citation is confirmed. I could not retrieve the abstract. From prior knowledge, committed pursuit invigorates action, and a person must disengage from an unreachable incentive before turning to others. Treat the content as *unverified*.

**Wrosch, Scheier, Miller, Schulz & Carver 2003.** Wrosch, C., Scheier, M. F., Miller, G. E., Schulz, R., & Carver, C. S. (2003). Adaptive self-regulation of unattainable goals: Goal disengagement, goal reengagement, and subjective well-being. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 29(12), 1494-1508. Three studies (N = 115, 120, 45). Both the ability to drop an unattainable goal and to take up another were linked to higher well-being, and the two interacted. Later reviews tie disengagement to lower distress and reengagement to positive well-being.

**Kurzban, Duckworth, Kable & Myers 2013.** Kurzban, R., Duckworth, A., Kable, J. W., & Myers, J. (2013). An opportunity cost model of subjective effort and task performance. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 36(6), 661-726 (pages as returned by search; the target article alone may end earlier, unverified). Felt effort tracks the value of the next-best use of limited control mechanisms, which pushes a person to switch. A theory with contested commentary, not a measured constant.

## 3. Engineering defaults

HF's numbers are engineering choices. None is fitted to data. The findings above support the shape of each rule, not its value.

| HF default | Shape it follows | Finding behind the shape |
|---|---|---|
| Calm urgency 0.35: projects form only below this need urgency | Play and self-set projects appear when the person is fed, safe and unstressed | Burghardt 2005; Little 1983 (projects need room beside pressing concerns) |
| Adopt every 30 min; cooldown 60 | A new project is considered on a slow clock, and not again soon after one ends | None. Pacing only |
| Temperament fit 0.25 + 0.75 x trait (openness) | Openness raises the pull toward new or varied projects; a floor keeps low-openness people from never starting | DeYoung 2015, as cited in the sibling note |
| Strength = importance x (0.4 + 0.6 x conscientiousness) | Importance and commitment raise pursuit; conscientiousness raises finishing | Locke & Latham 2002 (commitment moderates goal effects) |
| Stale 180 (minutes): drop a project not served | Disengage from a goal that gets no action | Klinger 1975 (unverified); Wrosch et al. 2003 |
| Half-life 120 (minutes): a project's completion value halves per 120 minutes of expected delay | The 2.3.0 temporal-goal discount, reused so 2.12 planning can value routes to a project | None. Reuses an existing HF shape |
| Pursuit bonus to serving options | A focal goal gains access and support | Shah et al. 2002; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 |
| Bounded switching cost to unrelated options | Leaving the current task costs something and is not blocked | Monsell 2003; Kurzban et al. 2013 |
| Cost scaled down by bodily urgency and fear; duties never taxed | Pressing needs and threat override shielding; duties are not options to shield from | Shah et al. 2002 (shielding is a moderated tendency); design rule, not a finding |
| Release after slack 20 (minutes unserved) or max 240 (total) | Goal activation decays without use; a pursuit must end so it cannot trap the person | Altmann & Trafton 2002; Wrosch et al. 2003; Klinger 1975 (unverified). Minutes are HF's own |
| Resume an unfinished thing | People tend to return to interrupted tasks | Ovsiankina 1928; Ghibellini & Meier 2025 |

The switching cost comes from millisecond lab trials and HF applies it to minutes-long choices. That is an analogy, not a measurement. The 20 and 240 minute limits have no source.

## 4. Not modelled

- **No world model.** A project is a label on perceived things, not a plan of the world.
- **No general planner.** Persistence biases the existing choice rule. It never builds multi-step plans. Bounded planning stays in HF 2.12.0.
- **No Zeigarnik memory boost.** The 2025 meta-analysis found no reliable recall advantage for unfinished tasks. An unfinished thing draws the person back (resumption), not into better recall.
- **No implementation-intention planning step.** The pursuit record is a cue-driven bias in the spirit of those findings, not an if-then plan the person writes.
- **No fitted effect sizes.** d about 0.65 and similar figures are not used as parameters.
- **No faith content.** Projects and persistence are psychological faculties. They add no lens or norm. Nothing about the ruh is computed, and no project is framed as religious duty or reward ([reconciliation.md](reconciliation.md)).
