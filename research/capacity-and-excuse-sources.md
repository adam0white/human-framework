# Capacity and excuse: prayers and fasts missed while not in control of oneself — source record

Research gathered 2026-10-04 by three subagent web passes and a direct re-read; written up the same day. Status: a sourced record of what named sources say, not a ruling and not a qualified Hanafi review. It answers the open question in the 2026-10-04 findings entry (`docs/findings.md`, engine 1.6.0, item 2): a villager held in a mental break books missed prayers and `salah` breaches, and whether a break should excuse a duty "needs a sourced ruling from `research/`".

Tiers as in [prayer-times-sources.md](prayer-times-sources.md): revealed / transmitted; classical Hanafi (**none read directly**; every title is "cited via" the page named, except that two fatwa pages print Arabic passages from Durr al-Mukhtar, Radd al-Muhtar and the two Muhits, and this note renders those into English itself; §2 and §6 lean on two such renderings); contemporary Turkish Hanafi (Diyanet, TDV); Hanafi fatwa sites (SeekersGuidance, islamqa.org mirrors, Darul Iftaa Chicago); other schools; engineering assumption.

**Quote caveats.** Unlike the earlier notes, most Turkish pages here were **read as raw page text** (fetched with curl and stripped of HTML, 2026-10-04), so Turkish in quotation marks marked "raw" is the page's own wording. The same holds for the islamqa.org and daruliftaa.us pages marked "raw". seekersguidance.org blocked the raw fetch (Cloudflare); its pages were seen only through the summarising fetch tool, so their "quotes" may be trimmed or reworded. Arabic classical passages appear only where a fatwa page prints them; English renderings of that Arabic are **this note's rendering**, not the page's. sunnah.com was not fetched (403 in earlier passes); hadith come from hadithunlocked.com and tohed.com, through the summarising tool. Qur'an wording is the Khattab translation on quran.com, through the same tool.

## 0. The two questions every source separates

The sources keep apart (a) **blame**: is the person sinful for not praying or fasting on time, and (b) **debt**: is the missed prayer or fast still owed as qada. Most excuses below lift (a) and leave (b).

| Claim | Tier | Source | Wording / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The hadith lifting error, forgetting and coercion from the community: Hanafi usul limits what is lifted to the otherworldly consequence (sin); the majority read it as lifting both | Contemporary (TDV); classical cited via | [TDV, "Nisyan"](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/nisyan) (Dönmez, 2007), raw | "Hanefî usulü ise anlamın uhrevî sonuçla yani günahın kaldırılmasıyla sınırlı sayılmasını gerektirir". English is this note's rendering. **The page's own English answer says "5 Salaah or more" are dropped. This note treats that line as an error against the page's own Arabic and the pages above.** |
| Ibn Umar was unconscious for more than a day and a night and did not make the prayers up; reasoning: short unconsciousness is like sleep (no release), long is like insanity or childhood (release), divided at a day and a night | Hanafi fatwa site; classical cited via | [Darul Iftaa Chicago, fatwa 257](https://daruliftaa.us/fatwa/257/), raw footnote | Arabic footnotes cite al-Muhit al-Radawi, K. al-Salat 1/372, and al-Muhit al-Burhani 3/33 (via page); a third bracketed passage's source is cut off in the extract. English summary is this note's. |
| "A person who is unconscious does not have to make up their prayers if they exceed six" | Hanafi fatwa site, summarising tool only | [SeekersGuidance: is prayer excused on a person who is unconscious](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/is-prayer-excused-on-a-person-who-is-unconscious/) | Looser wording than the rows above; Maraqi al-Falah cited (via page). |

**The threshold, resolved as far as the sources go.** Five prayers or fewer missed: owed. The debt drops once the time of a sixth prayer has *fully passed* within the episode (Muhammad's count by prayer times, called "the most correct" by Ibn Abidin per the muftionline page, and "completed, not entered" per TDV "Cünûn"). Abu Hanifa and Abu Yusuf, per TDV, count 24 hours by the clock instead. The muftionline English ("5 or more dropped") is an outlier against its own Arabic.

## 8. Engineering mapping (assumptions, not rulings)

Everything in this section is an **engineering assumption** about how a framework that represents a person's *understanding* of a norm could use the record above. None of it is a ruling. It proposes no code change; it records what a change would have to respect.

**Two axes.** The sources separate blame (breach, guilt) from debt (qada). The framework today has both pieces but joins them only for the fast: `agenda/` releases an exempted fast instance and records an `OwedMakeUp` (reasons `illness`, `travel`, `necessity`), while a missed prayer closes `broken`, books a `salah` breach when the norm is linked (`person.ts` missed-commitment path), and records no make-up. As read in the code (not probed per state), that path does not look at whether the person was asleep, downed, in a break or commanded. Per the sources, a near-universal feature is missing: in almost every case below a missed prayer **is still owed**.

| Framework state | Closest source category | Blame per sources | Debt per sources | Current behaviour | Right / wrong per the sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Asleep** through a prayer | Sleep (§4) | None if no intent to miss; sinful if one slept after the time entered while fearing not waking | Owed (qada), "when he wakes" | Missed + breach; no qada | Breach is wrong for the plain case; partly right only for the "slept after the time entered, expecting to miss" case, which the framework could read from whether the person chose sleep with the window open. Missing qada is wrong. |
| **Downed / unconscious** (`enableDowned`) | Ighma (§2) | None | Prayer: owed if five or fewer were missed; dropped once a sixth prayer time has fully passed within one episode (Muhammad's count; Abu Hanifa / Abu Yusuf count 24 hours). Fast: owed, by agreement (TDV) | Missed + breach; no qada | Breach is wrong. Debt needs a per-episode count of elapsed prayer times; a short episode owes, a long one releases. A downed but conscious person who cannot move the head is the illness case (§5) with the same count. |
| **Mental break** (`affect/crisis.ts`) | Not junun: the scope comment says it does *not* model psychiatric conditions or loss of reason, and the break still lets the person choose among the host's behaviours. So the nearest category is distress (§6). The engine's veto means the person cannot pick prayer during a break, so "accountable" here rests on how the scope comment frames the state (reason intact), not on what the vetoed person could do | Accountable: distress is not a sourced excuse; the test is retained capacity, and "motivation … is irrelevant" (Darul Iftaa Chicago) | Owed | Missed + breach; no qada | **Breach is what the sources support** for a break that leaves the person able to understand the obligation. If a host's break behaviour is meant to depict loss of reason (a person who cannot comprehend prayer), that is junun (§1): no blame, and the same five / sixth-time debt rule; the framework would need the host to say so per behaviour, since `crisis.ts` cannot tell. |

**What a change would need, if one is made.** (1) Missed prayers record an `OwedMakeUp` like exempted fasts, with a reason naming the source category (`sleep`, `unconscious`, …), so a person can carry and repay qada. (2) The breach is booked only where the person's understanding holds them at fault: awake, able, and choosing otherwise, or sleeping after the window opened while expecting to miss. (3) For downed or loss-of-reason states, a count of prayer times elapsed in the episode, releasing the debt once a sixth time has fully passed. (4) A fast missed while unconscious stays owed (TDV sets no limit and is silent on a whole-month case); one missed in loss of reason for the whole of Ramadan does not. Each is the engine's model of a person's understanding, with these rows as provenance; none computes acceptance.
