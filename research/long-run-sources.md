| Skill decay | Arthur, Bennett, Stanush & McNelly (1998), *Human Performance* 11(1), 57–101 | Meta-analysis (189 data points, 53 articles): substantial loss with nonuse, d from −0.01 right after training to −1.4 after more than 365 days; physical, natural and speed-based tasks decay less than cognitive, artificial and accuracy-based ones. Overlearning is **not** shown by this paper: its abstract does not mention it, 83 % of data points had no overlearning information, and the authors report only a weak effect that the limited data may have attenuated. That more practice slows decay is the standard hypothesis in the literature they review (e.g. Driskell et al. 1992, cited there, not checked here) | Verified against the paper's text (abstract and overlearning section) on 2026-10-04

Engine choices (assumptions): quality multiplies learning from 0.5× to 1.5× with ordinary practice at 1×; instruction adds up to 1× (twice the learning) from a fully engaged teacher 0.3 above the learner, as v0's guided practice did; observation learns at 0.3 of practice's rate toward 0.6 of the model's level. Consolidation (opt-in; the direction is the overlearning hypothesis noted above, not a finding of Arthur et al.): the forgetting half-life is multiplied by 1 + practice hours / 300, so a skill practised for years decays slowly; 300 hours is an assumption. Because learning per hour and forgetting both shrink with hours, a lifelong daily practitioner levels off at about 0.77 (docs/findings.md).

Engine choices (assumptions): HEXACO emotionality maps to the opposite of emotional stability; the HEXACO directions above are applied from age 18 at rates of 0.001–0.003 per year (a few hundredths over a lifetime on a 0..1 scale) and stop at 65; a year's lived experience (mean mood, commitments kept against broken, time with others, variety of activity) pushes the matching trait by at most 0.01 per year; each trait and value stays within 0.15 of its value when drift was enabled. The experience offset fades by 10 % a year (a pure engineering assumption: Ormel et al. favour experience-dependent or mixed set points and do not support a return to a fixed one; the fade only keeps the offset bounded), so sustained one-sided experience settles at about 0.1 from the anchor instead of pinning at the 0.15 bound, and a run of better years brings it back. Values follow the age pattern only (benevolence and tradition up, power, achievement, stimulation and self-direction down), at 0.001–0.002 per year. The cap keeps rank order mostly intact, consistent with high adult rank-order stability.
