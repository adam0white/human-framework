# Concealed charity: source record

Research verified 2026-10-26 for _The Left Hand_. This records sources and a fictional person's understanding. It is not a ruling or a qualified scholarly review. No runtime norm or API is added.

Tiers follow [janazah-and-taziyah-sources.md](janazah-and-taziyah-sources.md): **revealed** (Qur'an), **transmitted** (hadith), **classical interpretation cited via** a contemporary source, **contemporary interpretation**, and **engineering assumption**. The linked pages below were read through the web tool on this date, including the Arabic and English displayed for the verse and two hadith. No independent manuscript comparison, hadith-chain analysis or school-by-school survey was performed. All summaries below are paraphrases.

## 1. Revealed and transmitted sources

| Claim | Tier | Verified source | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public charity is good; concealing charity given to the poor is better | Revealed | [Qur'an 2:271](https://quran.com/al-baqarah/271), Arabic and Mustafa Khattab translation | The same verse praises open giving. It does not say every visible gift is ostentation. Its statement about expiation remains a transmitted belief, never a computed result. |
| Spending secretly and openly are both praised | Revealed | [Qur'an 2:274](https://quran.com/al-baqarah/274), Arabic and English | A reason to retain worthwhile public giving in the game. Reward belongs to God and is not simulated. |
| One of the seven people shaded is a donor who conceals charity so thoroughly that the left hand does not know what the right gave | Transmitted | [Sahih al-Bukhari 1423](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1423), Abu Huraira; Book 24, hadith 27 | The game's title refers to this wording. The report describes concealment and promised shade; it supplies no scoring formula or test of a donor's sincerity. |
| Muslim also transmits the seven-person report | Transmitted | [Sahih Muslim 1031a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1031a), Abu Huraira; Book 12, hadith 117 | This displayed Arabic and English reverse the hands: the right does not know what the left gave. Record the variation rather than silently harmonising it. Both reports describe concealment. No textual-critical explanation is claimed here. |

## 2. Interpretation and majority qualification

[Islamweb fatwa 281323](https://www.islamweb.net/amp/en/fatwa/281323/index.php) (2015) quotes al-Shawkani's *Fath al-Qadir*: the majority read 2:271's preference as concerning voluntary charity; a group extends it to obligatory charity. This is a **reported majority**, not an independently checked count of schools.

The same page cites Ibn Kathir and Ibn al-Arabi: concealment is generally preferable, while a stronger benefit can favour disclosure, including encouraging others. It associates concealment with avoiding display and protecting recipients from embarrassment. Those are interpretive reasons, not facts about every donor's motives. These classical works were **not read directly**. The contemporary page was read in full. Its other reports are not adopted here.

## 3. Held understanding and engineering boundary

Yusuf may hold the common reading that voluntary giving is generally better concealed. He may also understand that open giving is praised and can encourage help. This models **Yusuf's understanding**, not a ruling on the player or anyone else.

Who witnesses a gift, who repeats it, whether help arrives, and what a recipient says are ordinary social events. Any effects on trust, shame, pride or relationships are engineering assumptions about these fictional people. Disclosure alone cannot establish hypocrisy; anonymity cannot establish sincerity. The game must never compute divine acceptance, reward, expiation, promised shade or a quantity of ruh. Zakat rules, eligibility and duties are outside this record.
